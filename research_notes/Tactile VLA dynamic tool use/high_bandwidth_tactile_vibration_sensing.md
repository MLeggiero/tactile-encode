# High-Bandwidth Tactile, Vibration, and Acoustic Sensing for Robots — and ML Decoders for Contact / Tool-Engagement / Slip Events

Scope: sensing side of high-frequency physical events during tool use (hammering, drilling/driving), 2016–Sept 2026, emphasis 2022–2026. Numbers are quoted from primary sources where possible; "not stated" is used when a source does not give a value. Marketing claims are flagged as such. Items involving power tools, hammers, or impulsive impacts on a robot hand are flagged **[TOOL/IMPACT]**.

## KQ1. Sensor bandwidth table: native sampling rate and effective bandwidth of common robot tactile/vibration sensors; which can resolve a hammer-impact transient (~1–10 ms) or drill vibration (~50–300 Hz + harmonics)?

### Takeaway
Camera-based tactile sensors (GelSight Mini 25 fps, DIGIT 30–60 fps, Sharpa Wave 30 Hz) cannot resolve drill-frequency vibration or impact transients; the sensors that can are those with a dedicated high-rate channel: BioTac AC pressure (2.2 kHz), PapillArray (1 kHz), Evetac (1 kHz event stream, vibrations to 498 Hz), piezoelectric films (10 kHz), fingertip MEMS accelerometers (8 kHz), contact microphones (16–48 kHz), Digit 360's microphones/IMU ("vibrations up to 10 kHz"), and wrist F/T sensors (3.8–8 kHz). Magnetic skins (ReSkin 400 Hz, AnySkin 100 Hz as used, Xela 500 Hz) sit at the margin: enough to see a 50–200 Hz fundamental but not harmonics or the shape of a ~3 ms impact pulse.

### Inferences
- A hammer-nail contact pulse is on the order of a few ms (see KQ6), so its *shape* needs ≥~1–2 kHz bandwidth (BioTac PAC, PapillArray, PzE, accelerometer, contact mic, wrist F/T at kHz). Its *occurrence* is detectable at lower rates because the structure rings for tens of ms afterwards (cf. impact-aware papers recording "damped oscillatory responses" in joint velocities, KQ2), and MicCheck notes that a "knock" "tends to excite audible resonance" (KQ3).
- Drill/driver fundamentals of 50–300 Hz plus harmonics are within Evetac (498 Hz), PapillArray (1 kHz), BioTac, PzE, accelerometers and mics; magnetic skins at 400–500 Hz capture only the fundamental; camera-based sensors at 25–60 fps alias it.
- Digit 360 is the only commercial-ish fingertip that bundles an image, audio (kHz), IMU and pressure channel with an on-fingertip accelerator; the "1.2 ms reflex arc" is the only sub-5-ms fingertip-local sense-to-act latency found.
- Sensor vendors' "dynamic"/"material texture" claims (Sharpa DTA, PaXini 15 dimensions, Tacniq) are undocumented in rate/bandwidth terms and should be treated as unverified.

### Gaps
- Maximum hardware rate of AnySkin and pre-2026 Xela rates were not stated in fetched sources.
- Digit 360 per-channel rates (IMU, pressure) come from Sparsh-X's data pipeline, not a datasheet; camera-at-240-Hz vs 30-fps use is unexplained.
- Robotiq FT 300-S rate not verified (page 404).
- No datasheet-grade specs found for PaXini, Tacniq, Sharpa (rate), Figure, or Tesla hands.
- Contactile spec PDF could not be parsed; vibration figures come from the 2018 design paper snippet.

## KQ2. Joint effort as a high-frequency sensor: torque/current rates on common arms; impact or tool-engagement detection purely from proprioception

### Takeaway
Arm-level effort signals are available at 500 Hz–1 kHz (Franka 1 kHz with 13-bit joint torque sensors, Kinova Gen3 1 kHz, UR RTDE 500 Hz, Unitree G1 500 Hz/2 ms DDS), and there is a mature collision-detection literature (momentum observers; learned SVM/CNN detectors on motor current) that detects hard impacts from these signals, with reported latencies of ~64–96 ms on a 125 Hz lab robot; **[TOOL/IMPACT]** robots have hammered nails using only joint-torque/current sensing (Torobo, ARMADA), but no work was found that classifies *tool engagement* (drill bit engaged vs free-spinning, cam-out) from joint effort alone.

### Cited Findings
- Franka Emika: "13-bit resolution torque sensor to check joint torque", data and commands "transmitted via Ethernet protocols with sampling speeds up to 1 kHz" — [Sensors 2020 (PMC7435754)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7435754/); each joint "equipped with a high-resolution torque sensor (<0.05 N)" — [MDPI Sensors 2025 s25206315 (search summary)](https://doi.org/10.3390/s25206315). (libfranka doc pages returned 404 during research.)
- Universal Robots: RTDE output "as high as the real-time control loop frequency (500 Hz on e-Series and ur-Series robots)" — [UR RTDE guide](https://docs.universal-robots.com/tutorials/communication-protocol-tutorials/rtde-guide.html). In AURSAD, "The data from UR3e and screwdriver is sampled at 100Hz" via RTDE, and RTDE "will skip packages" if the controller is loaded — [AURSAD PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.01409).
- Kinova Gen3: "1 kHz closed-loop control", torque sensors in each actuator; Kortex low-level API allows joint-level position/velocity/torque/current control at 1 kHz — [Kinova FAQ](https://kinovarobotics.com/en/resources/gen3-faq); [Kinova spec sheet](https://static.generation-robots.com/media/feuille-specification-kinova-en.pdf).
- KUKA LBR iiwa: data collected at 1000 Hz in an iiwa pHRI framework — [arXiv 2502.02967](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.02967); FRI cycle rate not verified from KUKA docs in this research (lbr-stack README lists FRI versions only) — [lbr_fri_ros2_stack](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lbr-stack/lbr_fri_ros2_stack/humble/README.md).
- Unitree G1: dual encoders per joint feeding "a 500 Hz control loop with force-position control, torque limiting, and 2 ms DDS communication"; LowState/LowCmd over CycloneDDS; PMSM joints up to 120 N·m (EDU) — [Weston Robot G1 dev guide](https://docs.westonrobot.com/tutorial/unitree/g1_dev_guide/); ROS 2 joint states (position, velocity, estimated torque) published at 100 Hz — [unitree_ros2 DeepWiki](https://deepwiki.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_ros2/4.2.1-g1-overview). Raw motor-current stream rate: not stated.
- Figure/Tesla actuator effort rates: not stated anywhere found.
- Momentum-observer collision detection (Haddadin, De Luca, Albu-Schäffer): survey of model-based detection, isolation and identification with proprioceptive sensors, covering rigid and flexible-joint robots — [IEEE T-RO 2017, DOI 10.1109/tro.2017.2723903](https://doi.org/10.1109/tro.2017.2723903); the generalized-momentum external-torque observer was deployed on DLR LWR-III, KUKA LBR iiwa and Franka — [PMC7435754](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7435754/).
- Latency example (lab 3-joint cobot): 8 ms sampling (125 Hz); second-order momentum observer with low-pass/high-pass gains "to capture both fast impact and slow contact dynamics"; detection delays 0.080 s, 0.088 s, 0.096 s per joint, overall "0.064 s" to "0.096 s"; threshold 7.80 N·m; 100% detection over 50 randomized collisions — [PMC7435754](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7435754/).

Detection delay: not stated in abstract.
- Other observer variants: sliding-mode momentum observers — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333718782_Sliding_Mode_Momentum_Observers_for_Estimation_of_External_Torques_and_Joint_Acceleration); adaptive super-twisting — [JIRS 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10846-023-01814-5); variational-Kalman external torque estimation — [MDPI Sensors 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/20/6315); deep-Lagrangian-network momentum observer — [RCIM 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0736584525001474).

- **[TOOL/IMPACT]** Impact-aware manipulation (Saccon et al. line): post-impact velocity jump identified from simulation vs experiment with "3.1% average error" under reference-spreading control — [arXiv 2411.06319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06319); "18 experiments" of intentional surface impacts show recorded "damped oscillatory responses" in joint velocity that rigid-body models do not predict — [arXiv 2010.08220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08220); time-invariant reference spreading with an "interim-impact control mode", validated in "600 robotic hit-and-push and dual-arm grabbing experiments" — [arXiv 2411.09870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09870); multi-mode trajectory optimization validated "on the task of halting a large-momentum object" — [arXiv 2006.13374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.13374).
- **[TOOL/IMPACT]** Torobo humanoid hammering a nail: torque sensors at all arm/waist joints; "The instantaneous rebound force from the hammer is absorbed through a combination of the elasticity of the rubber material securing the hammer, the deflection in torque sensors and harmonic gears, backdrivability, and impedance control"; Cartesian impedance control; no tactile sensing mentioned — [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/innovation/torobo-humanoid-robot-hammers-nail).
- **[TOOL/IMPACT]** ARMADA low-cost bimanual arm ($6,100): T-motor AK70-10 / Cubemars RMD X4 V2 quasi-direct-drive actuators (1:10); no torque sensors — "sensor-free torque estimation" via a calibrated current-to-torque function; controller at 200 Hz, policy at 20 Hz; hammering: "an average of 10 strikes with a standard deviation of 1.26" to drive a nail 20 mm vs humans "13.15 strikes with a standard deviation of 6.41"; measured "average impact force of 50.5 N"; backdrivable joints "absorb impact forces through natural compliance" — [arXiv 2502.16908](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16908).
- **[TOOL]** Robotic (manual) screwdriving with a Shadow Hand on a Barrett WAM: no tactile sensors; contact forces estimated from strain-gauge joint torques via forward dynamics with "≤25% in magnitude and ≤30° in direction" error; screwdriver pose from Azure Kinect + ArUco; 20 trials, 17 exceeded five rounds of finger rolling/gaiting; failures when "the screwdriver's handle slipped off the fingers or its tip slid out of the screw's drive", with "vibrations of the Shadow Hand" cited as a contributing factor — [Tang, Jia, Xue, ICRA 2024 PDF](https://www.cs.iastate.edu/files/inline-files/icra24.pdf).
- **[TOOL]** Impact frequency of a down-the-hole drill identified from motor current signature analysis — [MDPI Applied Sciences 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/13/8/4650) (abstract-level; details not fetched).
- Hammering as a benchmark task spanning "free motion to hard contact" impedance range (robot-assisted haptic rendering) — [IEEE 2023](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10180217/).

### Inferences
- With 1 kHz torque/current streams, an impact's *occurrence* and *timing* are recoverable (momentum-observer residual spikes; post-impact velocity jumps), but the low-pass nature of gearboxes/observers plus 64–96 ms detection windows in typical implementations means joint effort is a poor channel for *waveform* features (e.g., drill harmonics) compared with fingertip vibration sensors.
- Both real-robot hammering demos (Torobo, ARMADA) rely on mechanical compliance (rubber, backdrivability, gear/torque-sensor deflection) rather than sensing to survive impacts; neither reports grip-force regulation from impact signals.
- Learned torque observers (NEXT/FACTR 2, Heo CNN) show that current-only arms can be made into usable contact sensors; combining a residual with a spectral detector could flag drill engagement, but this is untested in the literature found.

### Gaps
- No paper found that detects drill/driver engagement, cam-out, or bit slip from robot joint torque/current alone.
- Detection latency for Heo et al. and for Franka-native collision reflex were not obtained (paywalled / docs 404).
- KUKA FRI cycle time and humanoid (Figure/Tesla) actuator telemetry rates unverified.

## KQ3. Learned decoders for vibration/impact/audio signals in manipulation (features, architectures, tasks, and whether they involve tools or engagement states)

### Takeaway
The dominant recipe since 2022 is log-mel spectrogram → transformer (AST/SSAST, often AudioSet-pretrained) or small 2D CNN, run over 16 ms–3 s windows, fused with vision via attention and fed to diffusion/VLA policies; these decoders reliably detect contact modes, material/texture, and events hidden from vision. **[TOOL]** Tool-mediated cases exist (rod/fork with BioTac+NUSkin, spatula/eraser/tape gun in ManiWAV, Allen-key contacts in Sound of Touch), but no work was found decoding power-drill engagement/cam-out or hammer impacts on a robot hand.

### Cited Findings
- **Extended Tactile Perception (RSS 2021) [TOOL]** — BioTac micro-vibration (2.2 kHz) and NUSkin (4 kHz multi-taxel) sense vibrations propagating through held tools: contact localization "less than 1 cm on a 20 cm rod", grasp-stability prediction during handover, and "food identification using a standard fork"; conclusion: "multi-taxel vibro-tactile sensing at sufficiently high sampling rate led to the best performance across the various tasks and objects" — [arXiv 2106.00489](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00489).

### Inferences
- Feature pipeline consensus for kHz signals: high-pass (≈10–30 Hz) → log-mel or short-window FFT (5–16 ms hops) → small CNN or (SS)AST; AudioSet pretraining transfers to contact microphones (Hearing Touch, Sound of Touch, Audio-VLA), which suggests a pretrained audio backbone is a reasonable starting point for drill/hammer signatures.
- Latency budget is set by window length: event detectors use 16 ms windows (SlipSense) or 100 ms (Sound of Touch), whereas policy-conditioning encoders use 2–3 s history (ManiWAV, Hearing Touch) with ~0.23 s effective audio delay — the latter is fine for a slow VLA but not for a >200 Hz reflex loop.
- For the user's drill scenario, the closest evidence that audio/vibration distinguishes regimes is ManiWAV's velcro hook/loop and cup-contents sensing plus MicCheck's tap/knock/drag classes; the specific engaged/cam-out/free-spin regimes remain to be demonstrated.

## KQ4. Slip detection → grip-force regulation loops: state of the art, latencies, dynamic loads

### Takeaway
Closed-loop slip suppression on real grippers is now demonstrated at sensing rates of 1–10 kHz with detection delays of ~10–25 ms (PapillArray GRU: <10 ms; PzE film: 20.4 ± 6 ms; SlipSense: 76% within 23.1 ms) and full sense-to-force-peak latencies of ~50–185 ms; earlier camera-based (24 Hz, Dong 2019) and BioTac loops exist but are slower. All reported tests use quasi-static pulls, added weights, or motorized slides — none evaluate slip regulation under swinging-tool impulsive loads.

### Cited Findings
- Dong et al., ICRA 2019 (GelSlim): dense slip field from deviation of imprint motion from 2D rigid motion; incipient slip detected "at 24 Hz"; 10 objects × 24 trials → "86.25%" detection accuracy — [arXiv 1810.13381](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.13381); [code](https://github.com/siyuandong16/Incipient_slip_detection_with_GelSlim_sensor). Related closed-loop re-grasp with higher force threshold after slip — [GelSight PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5751610/).
- PapillArray GRU controller: 1 kHz pillar forces; incipient slip within 10 ms; online success 96.8%; OnRobot RG2 on UR5e; prevented slipping when relative translation reached "2.5 mm" and rotation "1.9°" — [arXiv 2307.04011](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.04011). Real-time friction estimation for grip-force control (Khamis et al., ICRA 2021) preprint could not be fetched — [Contactile preprint link](https://contactile.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/KhamisEtAl2021_ICRA2021_Manuscript_preprint.pdf).
- Evetac closed loop: 1 kHz sensing, 500 Hz gripper controller, ROBOTIS RH-P12-RN(A), 20 household objects — [arXiv 2312.01236](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.01236); "93%" grasp success — [IEEE via search](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel8/8860/4359257/10598389.pdf).
- SlipSense closed-loop re-grasp: "four operators perform 100 trials on 10 unseen objects … detecting all slip events in 100/100 trials without false alarms"; "detection-to-peak-force latency of ∼50 ms"; prevention in "95/100 trials"; model inference 2.3 ms; zero-shot UMI→Tesollo transfer 94.39% F1 (2-finger) / 87.24% (3-finger) — [arXiv 2609.15910](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15910).
- Reactive slip control in a four-finger tendon hand (ICRA 2026): slip onset "detected after 20.4 +/- 6 ms"; grasp-model update "<5 ms", internal-force selection "about 4 ms", theoretical response "on the order of 30 ms"; measured "grasp-reaction to slip-stop delay: 185 ± 27 ms" with "2.8 ± 1.5 mm" displacement; ground truth via motorized slide with ramp/step traction profiles and a tension sensor — [arXiv 2602.16127](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16127). Its related-work discussion cites prior "45–190 ms slip suppression end-to-end and 50–60 ms detection on force/torque signals" and notes chunked data transmission can inflate end-to-end latency to "100-400 ms" — [search summary of arXiv 2602.16127](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16127).
- FORTE (compliant fin-ray fingers with internal air channels): force 0–8 N with 0.2 N error; slip detected "within 100 ms", "93% accuracy"; 92% success on raspberries/potato chips — [arXiv 2506.18960](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18960); force estimation at 100 Hz (<10 ms) and slip algorithm at 500 Hz per search summary — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18960v1).
- BioTac slip prediction in the loop (PA-10 arm) stabilizes previously unknown objects — [Veiga et al., T-Haptics 2018](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8361482/).
- TacTip multi-fingered hand (T-MO): SVM slip detection; 11 objects; grasp stabilised when "adding weight to destabilise a grasp" — [arXiv 2010.01928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01928).
- Neuromorphic incipient slip (papillae + SCNN): 94.33% 3-class; ≥360 ms lead before gross slip; no closed loop reported — [arXiv 2509.09546](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09546).
- Whole-hand tactile reflexes at 1 kHz frame rate (ACTS): slip prevention and projectile detection via tactile-only reflexes — [arXiv 2410.13847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13847).
- Tactile-reactive grasp adjustment: 15-DoF arm-hand with 200 Hz spherical fingertip sensors; QP grasp adjustment "up to 200 Hz" with 1 kHz velocity tracking; stationary objects only, no disturbances — [arXiv 2509.15876](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15876).
- Physics-based slip detection from distributed contact forces — [arXiv 2109.11504](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.11504); friction-scaled vibrotactile feedback for slip in a supernumerary finger — [arXiv 2503.15447](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.15447); high-resolution touch across a whole hand for adaptive grasping — [Nature Machine Intelligence 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-025-01053-3).

### Inferences
- Reported loops separate into three tiers: (i) kHz-class contact sensors (PapillArray, PzE, accelerometer) → 10–25 ms detection; (ii) 500 Hz–1 kHz event/vision (Evetac, ACTS) → tens of ms; (iii) 24–60 fps cameras (GelSlim/GelSight) → ≥40 ms per frame, better suited for the slow policy layer.
- Even with 20 ms detection, actuator/transmission delays dominate (185 ms to slip-stop in the tendon hand; ~50 ms to peak force with SlipSense), so a hammer swing (impact every ~0.5–1 s, pulse ~ms) cannot be regulated within a strike; grip force must be set pre-impact from predicted load, with vibration/slip sensing used to correct between strikes.
- No study characterizes slip detectors under impulsive loads; false positives from impact-induced vibration (which look spectrally like slip) are an open risk for the user's use case.

- No dynamic-load (swinging/impacting tool) grip-regulation experiments found.
- Khamis et al. (real-time friction estimation, ICRA 2021) details unavailable (fetch returned empty).
- Xela-based slip/grip-control results not located within the remaining budget.

## KQ6. Impact-transient physics on the hand: grip force during hammering, transmitted vibration, ISO 5349 weighting, hammer/power-tool spectra

### Takeaway
Impact tools put most weighted energy below ~100 Hz (chipping hammers 10–40 Hz fundamentals; demolition hammers ~50 Hz blow rate + harmonics; hammer drills 10–100 Hz impact rate) but unweighted handle accelerations reach tens of m/s² with spectral content measured to 1250 Hz and pneumatic-hammer peaks near 500–570 Hz; a hammer–nail contact pulse is a few milliseconds. ISO 5349-1's Wh weighting (flat to ~16 Hz, falling to 1250 Hz) is a human-injury metric and should not be used to choose robot sensor bandwidth.

### Cited Findings
- ISO 5349-1:2001 defines the Wh frequency weighting mathematically; sensitivity "approximately proportional to acceleration below 16 Hz and decreases inversely with frequency from 16 to 1250 Hz"; prediction covers octave bands "8 Hz to 1000 Hz"; the standard is "provisionally applicable to repeated shock type excitation (impact)" — [Wh performance study (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232236438_Relative_Performance_of_Frequency_Weighting_Wh_and_Candidates_for_Alternative_Frequency_Weightings_for_Predicting_the_Occurrence_of_Hand-transmitted_Vibration-induced_Injuries); [Wh curve figure](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Frequency-weighting-curve-W-h-for-hand-transmitted-vibration-in-International-Standard_fig1_232236437); [EN ISO 5349-1:2001](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/cen/4550b076-4a96-4b51-848b-d51ad06f0e35/en-iso-5349-1-2001). A dedicated shock standard now exists: EN ISO 5349-3:2025 "Hand-Transmitted Vibration Shock Measurement" — [iTeh](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/cen/df55712d-702f-4a0f-b357-e80708bfa275/en-iso-5349-3-2025); ISO workshop on hand-transmitted shock standards — [MDPI Proceedings](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-3900/86/1/48). Wearable ISO-compliant HAV monitoring with transfer functions — [arXiv 2509.16536](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.16536).
- Chipping hammers: "fundamental vibration components in the 10–40 Hz frequency range", spectra examined over "25–1250 Hz"; "major resonances of the human wrist-arm system are primarily in the 10–40 Hz frequency range" — [CDC/science.gov summary](https://www.science.gov/topicpages/h/hand-arm+vibration+exposure); acute effects of chipping hammer vs grinder with measured grip and push forces — [Kihlberg et al. 1995 (PMC1128353)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1128353/); biodynamic response to impact hammer and grinder — [ScienceDirect 1994](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/016981419400060G).
- **[TOOL/IMPACT]** Riveting hammers (lab + field): ISO-weighted 1.46–5.22 m/s² (lab), 1.71–2.18 m/s² (field); unweighted lab average "68.81 m/s²", field 24.20–31.69 m/s²; measured in 24 one-third-octave bands "from 6.3 to 1250 Hz"; feed force standardized at "100 ± 20 N"; operators used "just enough grip force to maintain safe operation" (grip force not measured) — [PMC4677776](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4677776/).
- **[TOOL/IMPACT]** Electric demolition hammers: "extremely high vibration energy of about 50 Hz is related to the collision frequency of hammers" with peaks at upper harmonics; significant bit-dependent differences in "50–200 Hz" — [J. Low Frequency Noise, Vib. & Active Control 2023 (search snippet)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/14613484231186769).
- Small pneumatic hammer vibration shows "two peaks of almost equal height at frequencies of approximately 572 Hz and 498 Hz" — [arXiv 1410.4734 (search snippet)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1410.4734).
- **[TOOL]** Hammer drills: impact rate "typically in the range between 10 Hz and 100 Hz", optimal "25 Hz and 100 Hz"; loaded hammer drills ≥4500 BPM; rotary hammers 1,500–5,000 BPM — patents/vendor pages via search: [US 10906166](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10906166); [Pinshi blog](https://www.pspowertool.com/news/industry-news/how-does-the-rotary-hammer-drill-s-bpm-blows-per.html) (secondary sources).
- **[IMPACT]** Hammer–nail contact duration: textbook estimates of "approximately 2.86 milliseconds" (0.45 kg at 7 m/s) and "approximately 3.38 milliseconds" — [College Physics Answers](https://collegephysicsanswers.com/openstax-solutions/0450-kg-hammer-moving-horizontally-700-ms-when-it-strikes-nail-and-comes-rest); [Brainly example](https://brainly.com/question/28003644) (illustrative, not measured). Impact-hammer pulse durations "often measured in milliseconds" and dependent on tip material/geometry — [GlobalSpec](https://www.globalspec.com/learnmore/sensors_transducers_detectors/acceleration_vibration_sensing/impact_hammers) (page 403 on fetch; from snippet).
- **[IMPACT]** Robot-measured hammering impact: ARMADA reports "average impact force of 50.5 N" during nail driving, ~10× below a human palm strike (~575 N) — [arXiv 2502.16908](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16908).
- Impact-induced structural ringing: 18 intentional robot-arm impacts recorded as "damped oscillatory responses" in joint velocity — [arXiv 2010.08220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08220).
- Human sensing of hammer vibration scales with frequency, amplitude and duration; "higher frequencies having greater effects" — [vibration-dampening handle patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6311369).

### Inferences
- For sensor selection: to see the impact pulse itself, ≥2–5 kHz sampling (pulse ~3 ms ⇒ ≥~1 kHz bandwidth); to see the tool/hand ringing and power-tool harmonics, 1–2.5 kHz bandwidth (PzE film 30 Hz–2.5 kHz, BioTac ~1 kHz, PapillArray 1 kHz) is adequate; camera-based tactile is not.
- Unweighted handle accelerations of ~25–70 m/s² (riveting hammers) and higher for hand hammers imply fingertip accelerometers should have ≥±16 g range to avoid clipping; low-range consumer IMUs (±2–4 g) will saturate on impacts.
- Grip force during hammering is not quantified in the HAV literature found (operators "just enough grip"); pre-impact grip must be chosen from tool mass/velocity and friction, then corrected by slip sensing.

### Gaps
- No measured spectra of a *manual claw hammer* handle during nail driving found; no measured human grip-force profiles during hammering found.
- Consumer drill/driver and impact-driver vibration spectra (declared m/s² values, dominant Hz) not obtained (search budget exhausted).
- Full text of Kihlberg 1995 (grip/push forces, blow rate) not accessible.

## KQ7. Integration on real hardware: >200 Hz tactile/vibration/audio-to-actuation loops, stated latencies, and stacks (real-time ROS 2, EtherCAT, MCU/FPGA on the fingertip)

### Takeaway
Real systems closing ≥200 Hz loops from touch exist — Evetac (1 kHz sensing → 500 Hz gripper controller), ACTS (1 kHz tactile frames → whole-hand reflexes), SlipSense (240 Hz causal prediction, 2.3 ms inference, ~50 ms to peak force), PzE hand (10 kHz sensing, ~30 ms theoretical / 185 ms measured), PapillArray (1 kHz, <10 ms detection) and Digit 360's on-fingertip GAP9 (1.2 ms reflex) — while audio-conditioned policies run at 20–50 Hz with 0.2 s+ audio latency. Common stacks: on-sensor MCU/accelerator (Digit 360 GAP9, PapillArray, Xela CAN FD), EtherCAT/UDP F/T (Bota 3.84 kHz, ATI 7 kHz), DDS at 2 ms (Unitree), RTDE at 500 Hz (UR), 1 kHz torque interfaces (Franka, Kinova).

### Cited Findings
- Evetac: measurements accumulated for 1 ms, processed at 1000 Hz; gripper controller at 500 Hz — [arXiv 2312.01236](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.01236).
- Digit 360: GAP9 on-fingertip accelerator; "2x reduction in latency and jitter" vs off-board processing; "1.2 ms" event-to-action reflex arc; USB 3.0, up to 148 MBps — [arXiv 2411.02479](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.02479v1).
- SlipSense: causal prediction at 240 Hz; 2.3 ms model inference; 76% of slips within 23.1 ms; detection-to-peak-force ~50 ms; deployed on UMI gripper and Tesollo hand — [arXiv 2609.15910](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15910).
- PzE/PzR multifinger hand: 10 kHz PzE sampling; slip detection 20.4 ± 6 ms; grasp model <5 ms, force optimization ~4 ms; measured 185 ± 27 ms to slip stop — [arXiv 2602.16127](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16127).
- PapillArray: 1 kHz per-pillar 16-bit output; GRU detection within 10 ms; UR5e + OnRobot RG2 — [arXiv 2307.04011](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.04011).
- ACTS: 1,000 Hz frames from a 32×32 array; classification within 20 ms; tactile-only whole-hand reflexes (slip, sharp objects, projectiles) — [arXiv 2410.13847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13847).
- Tactile-reactive QP grasp control: tactile at 200 Hz is "the primary bottleneck"; QP at up to 200 Hz; 1 kHz joint velocity tracking — [arXiv 2509.15876](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15876).
- Sound of Touch: ~10 ms inference on 0.1 s audio windows on a KUKA LBR Med — [arXiv 2602.16846](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16846).
- Audio-in-the-loop policies: ManiWAV images at 20 Hz with 0.23 s calibrated audio delay — [arXiv 2406.19464](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19464); Hearing Touch actions at 30 Hz — [arXiv 2405.08576](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.08576); Audio-VLA at 25 Hz — [arXiv 2511.09958](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09958); MicCheck 50 Hz (camera-bound) — [arXiv 2511.18299](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18299).
- Transport layers: Xela CAN FD at 500 Hz — [Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/xela-robotics-adds-2-enhancements-uskin-sensor-family-ahead-robotics-summit/); Bota EtherCAT at 3840 Hz (8 kHz max) with integrated IMU — [botasys.com](https://www.botasys.com/force-torque-sensors); ATI UDP at 7000 Hz — [ATI](https://www.ati-ia.com/products/ft/ft_NetFT.aspx); Unitree DDS at 2 ms — [Weston Robot](https://docs.westonrobot.com/tutorial/unitree/g1_dev_guide/); UR RTDE 500 Hz with packet skipping under load — [UR docs](https://docs.universal-robots.com/tutorials/communication-protocol-tutorials/rtde-guide.html), [AURSAD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.01409); Franka 1 kHz — [PMC7435754](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7435754/); Kinova 1 kHz low-level — [Kinova FAQ](https://kinovarobotics.com/en/resources/gen3-faq).
- Measured collision-reflex latency on a 125 Hz lab arm: 64–96 ms — [PMC7435754](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7435754/).
- **[TOOL/IMPACT]** ARMADA hammering runs a 200 Hz controller under a 20 Hz policy, using current-based torque estimation — [arXiv 2502.16908](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16908); Torobo uses joint-torque impedance control with a state machine — [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/innovation/torobo-humanoid-robot-hammers-nail).

### Inferences
- The architecture the user proposes (slow VLA + fast low-level loop) matches every high-rate demonstration found: kHz sensing is consumed by a local detector (GRU/CNN/FFT-threshold) driving a 200–500 Hz gripper/force loop, while audio/tactile *representations* feed a 20–30 Hz policy.
- Achievable end-to-end today: ~10–25 ms detection + 30–50 ms actuation on parallel grippers; tendon hands add ~100+ ms. On-fingertip processing (Digit 360 GAP9, PapillArray MCU) removes USB/host jitter, which the Digit 360 paper quantifies as a 2× latency/jitter reduction.
- No system was found that closes a >200 Hz loop from vibration/audio during power-tool use; the closest are slip loops and SonicBoom/Vibro-Sense contact localization, which are open-loop perception.

### Gaps
- ROS 2 real-time / FPGA-specific tactile stacks with stated latencies were not found within budget (no primary source cited here for ROS 2-RT or FPGA fingertip preprocessing).
- Franka/KUKA native reflex latencies not verified from vendor docs.
- No published latency numbers for Digit 360 host-side (USB) pipeline beyond the "2x" relative claim.
