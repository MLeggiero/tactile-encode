# Humanoid / dexterous-hand robot tool use (2022–Sept 2026) and human grip-force control during tool use and impacts

Scope note: Part A covers robot demonstrations 2022–Sept 2026 (a few pre-2022 precedents are flagged as such). Part B covers human sensorimotor science of any era. Part C covers human tool-use / tactile datasets 2020–2026. Autonomy labels used throughout: **AUTONOMOUS** (learned or scripted policy running without a human in the loop, as stated by the source), **TELEOP**, **SCRIPTED** (hand-authored behaviors), **UNKNOWN** (source does not say). Research method note: web search budget was exhausted mid-task, so the later items were confirmed by direct page fetches of primary sources (arXiv, company pages, Europe PMC abstracts). Items I could not verify are listed under Gaps rather than asserted.

## Part A-1. What have the major US/Canadian humanoid companies (Boston Dynamics Atlas, Tesla Optimus, Figure, Sanctuary, Apptronik, Agility, 1X) shown for tool use, with what hands/sensors, and how much is learned vs. scripted vs. teleoperated?

### Takeaway
As of Sept 2026 none of the flagship Western humanoid programs has published a credible demonstration of hammering, drilling, or power-tool use; their learned-policy demos are packing/sorting, dishwasher loading, laundry and parts handling. Hands now carry tactile sensing (Figure 03: ~3 g fingertip threshold + palm cameras; Sanctuary: 5 mN micro-barometer pads; Tesla Gen-3: 22-DoF hand with "sensing glove"; Atlas: "tactile" listed on spec sheet), but the disclosed learned policies (Atlas LBM, Helix, TRI LBM) consume vision + proprioception only, at 10–30 Hz observation / up to 200 Hz action output.

### Cited Findings

Tasks: Spot-leg packing/sorting, rope tying, tablecloth spreading, t-shirt folding, 22-lb tire handling. No hammer, drill, wrench or screwdriver appears — [Boston Dynamics blog: Large Behavior Models and Atlas Find New Footing](https://bostondynamics.com/blog/large-behavior-models-atlas-find-new-footing/)
- The demonstration "used whole-body movements, such as walking, crouching and lifting, to accomplish a series of packing, sorting and organizing tasks" and "researchers interject unexpected physical challenges mid-task, such as closing the lid of a box" — [TRI news release](https://www.tri.global/news/ai-powered-robot-boston-dynamics-and-toyota-research-institute-takes-key-step-towards-general); [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/boston-dynamics-tri-use-large-behavior-models-train-atlas-humanoid/)

**Apptronik Apollo / Agility Digit / 1X NEO**

- No hammer/drill/screwdriver demo from Agility Digit or 1X NEO surfaced in any source consulted (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- The Western flagship stack (Atlas LBM, Helix, TRI LBM) is a slow diffusion/flow policy (7–30 Hz) issuing short action chunks to a high-rate joint controller (200 Hz–1 kHz), fed by vision + proprioception; tactile signals are physically present on Figure 03, Phoenix and Optimus but are not yet described as policy inputs in any published architecture. This is exactly the gap the user's proposed system targets.
- The most demanding contact tasks shown autonomously (dishwasher racks, metal parts, tire handling) involve quasi-static forces; nothing published exercises impulsive loads through a hand-held tool.

### Gaps
- No primary source describes Optimus, Atlas, Figure 03, Phoenix, Apollo, Digit or NEO hammering, drilling or driving screws (2022–Sept 2026). Absence in the sources I could reach is not proof of absence — short social-media clips may exist but were not verifiable here.
- Figure 03 hand DoF, Atlas production-hand DoF and taxel layout, and Optimus V3 taxel counts are not disclosed in any primary source I reached.

## Part A-2. What have Chinese and Japanese humanoid programs (UBTech, Unitree, AgiBot, Fourier, Galbot, Astribot, Xpeng, Tokyo Robotics) shown for hammering / drilling / screwdriving?

### Takeaway
The only vendor-stated power-tool capability is UBTech's claim that Walker S "can use an electric screwdriver to tighten screws on car doors"; the only verified hammering videos are Tokyo Robotics' Torobo (2024, impedance-controlled, autonomy unstated) and the nail-hammering event at the 2nd World Humanoid Robot Games (Beijing, Aug 2026), where most nails went in crooked and autonomy was not reported.

## Part A-3. Academic humanoid / dexterous-hand tool use (hammer, screwdriver, articulated tools): which hands, which sensors, learned vs. scripted, and what rates?

### Takeaway
Real-robot dexterous-hand hammering and screwdriving now exist as *learned* policies but at low bandwidth and mostly without tactile input: SimToolReal swings a hammer with a 22-DoF Sharpa hand at 30 Hz from object pose only; TeleDexter drives real nails via 30 Hz teleop and its behavior-cloned policy reaches 73% on the hammer task; Tool-as-Interface hammers 13/13 from RGB-only human video; DexScrew is the standout that uses dense fingertip tactile (120 three-axis elements per fingertip) for screwdriving at 20 Hz. Impact regulation is still handled by passive compliance/impedance (Torobo) or optimal control (iLQR), not by learned grip-force policies.

### Cited Findings
- **DAPG / Adroit hammer (2017, simulation precedent):** 24-DoF ADROIT hand; task "pick up and hammer with significant force to drive the nail into the board. Nail position is randomized and has dry friction capable of absorbing up to 15N force"; demos via "CyberGlove III ... HTC vive tracker"; 25 demos; hammer task reached 90% success in "55 RL iterations" ≈ "6.1 robot-equivalent hours", ~30× more efficient than shaped-reward RL — [Rajeswaran et al., arXiv 1709.10087](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1709.10087)
- **Tool-as-Interface (2025):** Kinova Gen3 and UR5e arms (parallel grippers, not dexterous hands); human demos from "two RGB cameras" with bare hands; nail-hammering "requiring the robot to locate the nail, draw back the hammer, and strike the nail tip accurately"; success "13/13" vs "0/13" for teleop-trained diffusion policy; sensing "only RGB inputs from RealSense D415 cameras", no force/tactile — AUTONOMOUS — [arXiv 2504.04612](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04612v2); data-collection time reduced "77% and 41% compared to teleoperation and the state-of-the-art interface" — [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04612)
- **SimToolReal (Stanford, 2026):** "22-DoF Sharpa five-fingered left hand mounted on a 7-DoF KUKA iiwa 14 arm"; single RL policy trained on procedurally generated tool-like primitives; 6 tool categories × 24 tasks including Hammer ("Grasp, 90° in-hand rotation, swing down or side") and Screwdriver ("Grasp, 90° in-hand rotation, spin in free space"); policy inputs "proprioception, object pose, grasp bounding box, and goal pose" — no tactile/force; pose estimation at 30 Hz; "120 real-world rollouts spanning 24 tasks, 12 object instances, and 6 tool categories"; outperforms retargeting/fixed-grasp baselines by 37% — AUTONOMOUS, but the hammer "swing" is a free-space goal-pose sequence, not a nail-driving impact task — [arXiv 2602.16863](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16863)
- **TeleDexter (Tsinghua/PKU/BIGAI, July 2026):** LEAP hand (16 DoF) and SharpaWave (22 DoF) on a Franka FR3; "All teleoperation evaluations run at 30 Hz"; HammerUse task (7 stages) "drove the two nails into the board"; ScrewdriverUse requires "axial alignment with the screw and sustained in-hand rotation"; TELEOP success 75% average (66.7–86.7%); Diffusion-Policy BC from 50 demos: "HammerDriver: 73.3%", BulbInstall 46.7%, BrushForward 40.0%; no tactile sensing, causing "tracking stall" failures — [arXiv 2607.11481](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11481v1)
- **DexScrew (UC Berkeley, Dec 2025):** XHand, 12 DoF (thumb/index 3 DoF each, others 2 DoF) on UR5e; "Each fingertip is equipped with a pressure-based tactile array comprising 120 sensing elements, each measuring three-axis forces" (0.05 N minimum; 1,800 tactile values/step); PPO in IsaacGym (8,192 envs, 1.5B steps); control 20 Hz; RL skill used inside teleop to collect real demos, then tactile behavior cloning (K=5 history, H=16 horizon); real-world progress ratio "95.00±13.24" % for screwdriving (avg rotation time 187.9 s) and 95–99% across nut shapes; generalizes to unseen nut/screwdriver geometries — AUTONOMOUS with tactile — [arXiv 2512.02011](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02011v1)
- **In-hand manipulation of articulated tools (Atar, Huang, Richter, Yip, Sept 2025):** scissors, pliers, surgical instruments, staplers; "whole-hand tactile and force-torque feedback" fused via cross-attention; sim-trained base policy + hardware-demo refinement; "robust sim-to-real transfer, improved disturbance resilience" — AUTONOMOUS with tactile + F/T — [arXiv 2509.23075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23075)
- **DexterityGen (2025):** learned low-level controller that "refines a coarse motion by a high-level policy such as teleoperation to fine dexterous actions"; tasks: pen, screwdriver, syringe, assembly; "DexGen can reject dangerous actions"; TELEOP high level + learned low level; no tactile mentioned — [DexterityGen project page](https://zhaohengyin.github.io/dexteritygen/); a 2026 survey summarizes it as "large-scale RL pretraining with a diffusion-based controller" for "rotation, regrasping, and complex tool use" — [Survey, arXiv 2605.13925](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.13925v2)
- **ScrewMimic (UT Austin, RSS 2024):** bimanual screw-motion skills from a single RGB-D human video; extracts wrist trajectories and contact points, then "iteratively refines using an autonomously generated reward signal"; no force sensing — AUTONOMOUS after self-supervised refinement — [ScrewMimic](https://robin-lab.cs.utexas.edu/ScrewMimic/)
- **Impact-aware hammering via optimal control (2024):** 7-axis arm nail-hammering with "iLQR ... with ... ADMM" maximizing directional velocity manipulability; no learning — SCRIPTED/planned — [arXiv 2402.05502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05502)
- **Sim-to-real RL on humanoids (NVIDIA/Berkeley, CoRL 2025):** multi-fingered humanoid; tasks "grasp-and-reach, box lift and bimanual handover"; no tools — [arXiv 2502.20396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20396)
- **TRI LBM paper (July 2025):** bimanual Franka FR3 with parallel grippers (WSG50-110), 10 Hz control, inputs "end-effector poses ..., continuous gripper width, 6 RGB images, and one natural-language instruction" — no F/T or tactile; ~1,695 h demos; unseen long-horizon tasks include CutAppleInSlices ("use an apple corer to core an apple, retrieve a knife from a crock, unsheath the knife") and BikeRotorInstall; 1,800 real trials; finetuned LBM beats single-task baseline in 4/5 real tasks; needs "<30% of the data" — AUTONOMOUS, hand-tool (knife/corer) but no power tools — [arXiv 2507.05331](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05331v1)
- **IHMC loco-manipulation system (Sept 2026):** Unitree H1-2 and "Alex" humanoids; behaviors are authored with "object-centric Affordance Templates" plus a whole-body controller; "push door traversal in 34 seconds and sort six balls by color in 45 seconds"; no tools — SCRIPTED — [arXiv 2609.01518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01518)
- **Force-regulating sim-to-real hand policy (Jan 2026):** five-finger hand; "dense virtual tactile units" in sim; "current-to-torque calibration ... mapping motor current to joint torque"; first "controllable grasping on a multi-finger dexterous hand trained entirely in simulation and transferred zero-shot"; grasp-force tracking + in-hand reorientation (no tools) — [arXiv 2601.02778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02778)
- **Survey framing:** tool use demands "reasoning about functional affordances rather than mere object geometry" and generating "precise force interactions"; RL dominates contact-dynamics learning, IL for few-shot generalization, VLAs for tool semantics — [arXiv 2605.13925](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.13925v2)

### Inferences
- Across all learned real-robot hammer/screwdriver results, the control loop is 20–30 Hz and success is measured by geometric progress (nail depth, rotation), not by force regulation; none reports grip-force or impact-force traces. A high-frequency low-level loop that modulates grip during impact has no published learned counterpart on a humanoid hand.
- DexScrew's recipe (sim RL for finger gait → skill-assisted teleop to collect tactile data → tactile BC) is the closest existing template for a "slow policy + tactile-aware fast primitive" design, but its task (manual screwdriver) has no impulsive loads.

### Gaps
- No paper found that uses a *powered* drill/driver with a dexterous hand and a learned policy; the DexScrew screwdriver is manual.
- Control rates for Tool-as-Interface and the Atar et al. articulated-tool policy are not stated in the reachable text.

## Part A-4. What dual-system / tactile-VLA architectures and rates are documented that bear on a "slow VLA + fast tactile controller" design?

### Takeaway
Published dual-system stacks run the slow module at 7–10 Hz and the fast module at 50–200 Hz (Helix 02 adds a 1 kHz "System 0"); tactile-augmented VLAs from 2025–2026 (VLA-Touch, AT-VLA, TacForcing) inject touch at the fast stream with reported closed-loop latencies down to 40 ms, but none is demonstrated on impact or power-tool tasks.

### Cited Findings
- Helix: S2 VLM 7–9 Hz, S1 200 Hz, 35-DoF action space — [Figure: Helix](https://www.figure.ai/news/helix); Helix 02: S1 200 Hz, S0 1 kHz (10M params) — [Figure: Helix 02](https://www.figure.ai/news/helix-02)
- NVIDIA GR00T N1: "System 2 reasoning module is a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) that runs at 10Hz on an NVIDIA L40 GPU"; System 1 diffusion transformer "generates closed-loop motor actions at a higher frequency (120Hz)"; chunk "H=16"; deployed on Fourier GR-1; human-video pretraining sources include Ego4D, Ego-Exo4D, Assembly-101, EPIC-KITCHENS, HOI4D, HoloAssist, RH20T-Human, covering "grasping, tool use, cooking, assembly" — [arXiv 2503.14734](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14734)
- Physical Intelligence π0: "we use H=50", "frequencies of up to 50 Hz for dexterous tasks", 300M-parameter action expert (3.3B total), "over 10,000 hours of robot data" across "7 different robot configurations and 68 different tasks" — [arXiv 2410.24164](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.24164)
- Atlas LBM: 450M DiT + flow matching, 30 Hz observations, 48-step chunks — [Boston Dynamics](https://bostondynamics.com/blog/large-behavior-models-atlas-find-new-footing/)
- AT-VLA (May 2026): "a slow visual-language stream for low-frequency perceptual reasoning and a fast tactile control stream for high-frequency physical interaction understanding", "real-time close-loop responses within 0.04 s", adaptive tactile injection "only when it significantly contributes to action generation" — [arXiv 2605.07308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07308)
- TacForcing (Aug 2026): "a streaming action expert to generate actions conditioned on the evolving tactile observations acquired during execution"; Execution-Aware Tactile Attention "restricts tactile conditioning to actions nearing execution"; 65% avg on six UniVTAC sim tasks, 69% on three real contact-rich tasks — [arXiv 2608.25798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25798)
- VLA-Touch (July 2025): "a pretrained tactile-language model for semantic tactile feedback and a diffusion-based controller that refines VLA-generated actions with tactile signals" — [arXiv 2507.17294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17294)
- TaF-VLA (Jan 2026) targets "Tactile-Force Alignment in Vision-Language-Action Models for Force-aware Manipulation" (title only verified) — [arXiv 2601.20321](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.20321)
- Meta Digit 360 fingertip: "over 8 million taxels", "forces as small as 1 millinewton", "on-device AI accelerator"; Digit Plexus integrates "Digit, Digit 360, and ReSkin across fingertips, fingers, and palm"; Sparsh encoder pretrained on "over 460,000 tactile images"; Wonik Robotics building an Allegro Hand "fully integrated with tactile sensing leveraging Meta Digit Plexus" — [Meta FAIR blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/fair-robotics-open-source/); [GelSight](https://www.gelsight.com/gelsight-and-meta-ai-introduce-digit-360-tactile-sensor/)

### Inferences
- The 40 ms closed-loop figure from AT-VLA is roughly half the human cutaneous grip-reflex latency (74 ± 9 ms; Part B-2), so a tactile fast stream at ≥25 Hz already matches human reactive timescales; matching *predictive* grip scaling (~150 ms before impact) is a question of the slow stream's forward model, not the fast stream's latency.

### Gaps
- None of the tactile-VLA papers reachable here reports an impact, hammering or power-tool task; their tasks are contact-rich but quasi-static.

## Part B-1. Anticipatory (feedforward) grip-force control: grip–load coupling and internal models

### Takeaway
Humans scale grip force in parallel with — and slightly ahead of — load force using a forward model of the arm/object dynamics (Johansson & Westling 1984; Flanagan & Wing 1997), keeping a small friction-dependent safety margin; the coupling holds for inertial, viscous, elastic and composite loads and is present with essentially zero lag when loads vary smoothly.

### Cited Findings
- Johansson & Westling 1984 (precision-grip lifting): "The grip force changed in parallel with the load force generated by the subject"; "the more slippery the object the higher the grip force at any given load force" with a relatively small safety margin; adaptation "was dependent on cutaneous afferent input" (local anaesthesia abolished it); cutaneous influence on force appears ~0.1 s after initial grip and slip-triggered corrections within 0.06–0.08 s — [Johansson & Westling 1984, Exp Brain Res 56 (abstract via Europe PMC)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00237997)
- Three-phase lift structure (preload, loading, hold) and the fact that "grip force-load force coupling entails anticipation of load force changes" — [Springer: Grip force control of predictable external loads](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00221-007-1195-6); the coupling "is a well-studied example of a forward internal model" that "can even slightly lead load force" — [J Neurophysiol 2020: Grip force anticipation of nonlinear, underactuated load force](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/jn.00616.2020)
- Flanagan & Wing 1997: manipulandum on servo-controlled linear motors creating inertial, viscous, elastic and composite loads; "Grip force was modulated in parallel with, and thus anticipated, fluctuations in load force" in all conditions and subjects; conclusion: the CNS predicts load force with an internal model — [J Neurosci 17(4):1519](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/17/4/1519); the earlier point-to-point study is Flanagan & Wing 1993 — [Exp Brain Res](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00229662)
- Hore et al. 2001 (skilled overarm throws): throwers grip "with a force proportional to ball weight and intended ball speed" during the backswing and increase grip "throughout the backswing and forward throw"; after release "the finger flexed in proportion to the peak force on the finger before ball release" — a fully anticipatory strategy for a self-generated dynamic load — [J Neurophysiol 86(6):2678](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/jn.2001.86.6.2678)
- Naik & Ambike 2025 (why hammers go in the dominant hand): the dominant hand shows "stronger grip-load coupling, indicating superior predictive control" while the non-dominant hand "stabilized the object better and exerted a higher grip force"; task difficulty had minimal effect — [PLoS One 2025 (via Europe PMC)](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=hammering%20AND%20%22grip%20force%22&resultType=core&format=json&pageSize=8)
- Tool-mediated elastic force fields: peak grip force is synchronized (≈zero delay) with load for "soft" fields but lags by ~60 ms for "stiff" fields, with a strategy switch at roughly 100–200 N/m — [Frontiers in Neurorobotics 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurorobotics/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2018.00031/full)
- Grip–load coupling can be intermittent during oscillation of a hand-held object — [Exp Brain Res 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00221-018-5315-2)

### Inferences
- For a robot, the analogue is: grip-force command = f(predicted load from the planned arm motion + tool inertia) with a friction-scaled margin; the prediction must be available before motion onset (it is not a reaction to measured load).
- Naik & Ambike's dominant/non-dominant asymmetry maps naturally onto a bimanual hammering policy: the tool hand runs predictive grip-load coupling; the nail-holding hand runs a higher-margin stabilization grip.

### Gaps
- Subject counts (N) for the classic Johansson & Westling and Flanagan & Wing papers were not in the abstracts I could retrieve.

## Part B-2. Grip responses to impacts and collisions: what is predictive, what is reflexive, and on what timescales (ms)?

### Takeaway
Impulsive loads rise in <20 ms, so only prediction can protect the grasp: humans begin a programmed grip increase ~150 ms before a self-produced impact, scale it to expected ball/hammer mass, drop height and friction, and show a stereotyped grip peak ~60–65 ms *after* peak impact load; reflexive (cutaneous/proprioceptive) grip corrections arrive at 60–90 ms latency (74 ± 9 ms to slip), which is too late to prevent a slip but restores the safety margin afterward.

### Cited Findings
- Johansson & Westling 1988 (ball dropped into hand-held cup, self- vs experimenter-released): preparatory actions occurred "ca. 150 ms prior to the impact" — grip increase plus lifting and "general stiffening of the hand/arm system" — and were scaled to ball weight, apparatus weight, drop length and friction; "automatic muscle responses triggered by the impact" appeared "too late to prevent slips" but restored safety margins, and were "most pronounced during unexpected load changes" — [Johansson & Westling 1988, Exp Brain Res 71:72–86](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00247523)
- Johansson & Westling 1987: slip → grip-ratio upgrade latency "74 +/- 9 ms", signalled by FA I, FA II and SA I afferents; "about half the minimum latency for intended grip force changes triggered by cutaneous stimulation" — [PubMed 3582528](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3582528); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00236210)
- Cole & Abbs 1988 (load pulses applied to a grasped object): "Grip force increases occurred consistently from 60 to 90 ms after onset of the load force increase", lasting 100–200 ms, scaled to load magnitude/velocity but independent of baseline grip; responses "were abolished or substantially reduced when loads were delivered directly to the hand rather than to the object" — [J Neurophysiol 60(4)](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/10.1152/jn.1988.60.4.1513)
- Delevoye-Turrell, Li & Wing 2003: in collisions load force rises "in less than 20 ms", so "only predictive control mechanisms can compensate"; grip is more efficient (lower for the same load) when the collision is "actively produced than when receiving the collisions"; active collisions improve with repetition, imposed ones do not — [Q J Exp Psychol A 2003](https://doi.org/10.1080/02724980245000025)
- Turrell, Li & Wing 1999 (hand-held object striking / struck by a pendulum): "subjects increased GF in advance of the collision"; the "anticipatory-GF magnitude response was scaled to the predicted LF magnitude", with stronger scaling for self-produced impacts; when receiving without advance information subjects set grip "to the mid-range of the experienced forces" — [Exp Brain Res 128:86–91](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s002210050822); [PubMed 10473745](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10473745/)
- White et al. 2011 (targeted tapping with impacts): "maximum grip force occurred approximately 65 milliseconds after peak load at contact" with a "stereotyped profile" indicating pre-programming; grip and load are "coupled in phase" during transport; the CNS uses grip force to regulate both stiffness and damping of the hand — [Neuroscience 189:269–276](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0306452211004957)
- Serrien et al. 1999 (drawer pulled to a mechanical stop): grip rises with load at movement onset, then "a proactive grip force increase prior to the time of impact"; adults "calibrated the second phase of the grip force profile in the initial trials"; children instead "used a probing strategy" — [Exp Brain Res 124:100–106 (via Europe PMC)](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE%3A%22Grip%20force%20adjustments%20induced%20by%20predictable%20load%20perturbations%22&resultType=core&format=json&pageSize=2)
- Kuling, Salmen & Lefèvre 2019 (robotic simulated collisions): "the temporal onset of the grip force build up did not depend on the impact load"; grip peak timing was "independent of the impact load", i.e., "time-locked planning of the complete grip force profile" with slip prevented by adjusting magnitude and rate; learning transferred between hands after a single collision — [Exp Brain Res 237(10) 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00221-019-05606-y)
- Review of impact tasks (drop, drawer-stop, striking a surface): "a maximum of grip force approximately 60 ms after peak load force"; catch trials with no load prove the pattern is "feedforward" — [Frontiers in Neurorobotics 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurorobotics/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2018.00031/full)
- Grip responses to imposed load perturbations are independent of object dynamics — [J Neurophysiol 2008](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/jn.90593.2008)

### Inferences
- Timescale map for a controller: (i) predictive ramp starting ≥150 ms before expected impact, scaled by an internal model of tool mass, swing velocity and friction; (ii) a time-locked profile whose peak lands ~60 ms after the impact peak (co-contraction/impedance during the post-impact ringing); (iii) a 60–90 ms reactive loop triggered by slip/vibration signals that raises the safety margin for the *next* stroke rather than rescuing the current one. A robot fast loop at 100–200 Hz with tactile slip detection can beat (iii) but must still implement (i) and (ii) from prediction.
- The White et al. finding that grip force sets both stiffness and damping justifies treating grip force as an impedance-shaping command in an impact controller, not just a friction-margin variable.

### Gaps
- N subjects are not stated in the abstracts retrieved for Johansson & Westling 1988, Cole & Abbs 1988, Turrell 1999, White 2011 or Kuling 2019.
- No study retrieved measures grip force during repeated *hammer* strikes with a force-instrumented handle in a neuroscience setting; the impact literature uses balls, drawers, pendula and tapping.

## Part B-3. Grip force during hammering and power-tool use (ergonomics / biomechanics): magnitudes, hammer mass effects, vibration

### Takeaway
Ergonomics data give the force envelope: hammer-drill operators hold the main handle with median grip 84–156 N while pushing with 100–200 N feed force, and higher grip reduces transmitted vibration; doubling hammer mass (1→2 lb) raises shoulder/elbow/wrist moments 50–150 %, most in the cocking and strike/follow-through phases; hand-held stone-hammer impacts measured with an in-tool sensor peak at ~135–730 N.

### Cited Findings
- Lindenmann et al. 2021 (hammer drills, N = 15 professional power-tool test personnel): main-handle median grip force 84–156 N, auxiliary handle 10–30 N; feed forces tested 100, 150, 200 N; "RMS of the acceleration and frequency weighted acceleration decrease" with increased gripping force; body posture significantly affects main-handle RMS acceleration (p = .003, r = .367) — [Applied Ergonomics 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003687021000776)
- Balendra & Langenderfer 2017 (N = 10; 1-lb vs 2-lb hammer): "the larger hammer caused substantial increases (50-150%) in moments" at shoulder rotation, elbow varus-valgus and pronation-supination, wrist radial-ulnar deviation and rotation; "Moment increases were greater in cocking and strike/follow-through phases as opposed to swinging"; trial and phase durations and cocking-phase variability increased with the heavier hammer — [Applied Ergonomics 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003687016302617)
- Sensorized hammerstone (knapping): pneumatic sensing chamber + Honeywell 60 psi pressure sensor sampled at 100 kHz, validated against a 1 kHz force plate; peak strike forces from ~135 N to 730 N with RMS error under 6%; 180 validated strikes + 60 flaking strikes; data in the paper's supporting information — [PLoS ONE via PMC11407656](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11407656/)
- Pistol-grip power tools (cadaver study, 5 N·m reaction torque): "The contribution of the grip force to the tendon strain and median nerve pressure was greater than the contribution from the reaction torque"; peak tendon strains 1.5–2 % — [Bakker et al. 2022, IJOSE (via Europe PMC)](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=(kickback%20OR%20%22reaction%20torque%22)%20AND%20(%22power%20tool%22%20OR%20drill%20OR%20grinder)%20AND%20(%22reaction%20time%22%20OR%20%22grip%20force%22)&resultType=core&format=json&pageSize=6)
- Higher gripping force raises the rotational mechanical impedance of the hand–arm system under vibration — [Applied Ergonomics 2021 (search summary)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003687021000776)
- Industrial screwdriving: with a high-quality clutch and short shut-off time "most of the reaction torque on hard joints will be absorbed by the tool mass with hardly any jerk to the operator's hand" — [Atlas Copco Pocket Guide to Screwdriving](https://www.atlascopco.com/content/dam/atlas-copco/industrial-technique/general/documents/pocketguides/1007%2001Pocket%20Guide%20to%20Screwdriving.pdf)
- Ergonomic hammer handles are designed "to accommodate a larger friction surface for the power grip" with palm-support anchor points — [Springer review: grip force, ergonomic interventions and task performance](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12008-024-02145-x)
- Tennis-grip work shows grip characteristics alter racquet vibration response (analogous handle-vibration coupling) — [Springer: Relating Grip Characteristics to the Dynamic Response of Tennis Racquets](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-0-387-46051-2_28)

### Inferences
- Design numbers for a humanoid hand holding a hammer drill: sustained grip ~100–150 N on the main handle plus 100–200 N axial push, i.e., well above the 20 N fingertip force of Sharpa Wave or the pinch forces of most tendon-driven hands — this is a power-grasp/palm-load problem, not a fingertip problem, which argues for palm tactile coverage and wrist F/T sensing.
- The hammer-mass result (joint moments rising fastest in cocking and strike/follow-through) indicates the post-impact "ringing" phase is where torque regulation matters most.

### Gaps
- I found no laboratory study reporting grip force (N or %MVC) on an instrumented *claw-hammer* handle during nail driving, nor grip force vs. hammer velocity; the hammer literature retrieved reports joint moments and kinematics only.
- Subject-level numbers on grip force during electric drill or impact-driver use (as opposed to hammer drills) were not found.

## Part B-4. Tool embodiment (body schema), sensing through a tool, and how humans detect drill/screw engagement

### Takeaway
Tools are incorporated into the body schema (bimodal parietal neurons extend their visual receptive fields along a rake; grabber use lengthens the represented arm), and texture/contact information is read through a hand-held probe via vibration (roughness correlates 0.92 with vibratory power); for drills, surgeons rely on "the feel of the drill" and react to breakthrough in ~0.23 s (overshoot ~3.5 mm), versus 0.02 s with automated haptic detection — evidence that vibration/force sensing at the grip plus reaction-time-free prediction is what a robot must replicate.

### Cited Findings
- Iriki, Tanaka & Iwamura 1996 (macaque rake use): bimodal postcentral neurons' "visual receptive fields were altered to include the entire length of the rake or to cover the expanded accessible space"; "a tool is an extension of the hand in both a physical and a perceptual sense" — [Neuroreport 1996 (via Europe PMC)](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE%3A%22Coding%20of%20modified%20body%20schema%20during%20tool%20use%22&resultType=core&format=json&pageSize=2)
- Maravita & Iriki 2004 ("Tools for the body (schema)"): extended motor capability is followed by changes in networks holding "an updated map of body shape and posture (the putative 'Body Schema' of classical neurology)" — [Trends Cogn Sci 2004](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1364661303003450)
- Cardinali et al. 2009: using a mechanical grabber that extends the arm "does alter the kinematics of subsequent free-hand grasping movements, and this effect is driven by an increase of the represented length of the arm", i.e., "what is modified is the somatosensory representation of intrinsic properties of the body morphology"; 423 citations — [Current Biology 2009](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/comments/S0960-9822(09)01109-9); citation count via [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/j.cub.2009.05.009?fields=title,abstract,year,authors,citationCount)
- Proprioception is necessary for this plasticity (deafferented patient) — [Frontiers Hum Neurosci 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2016.00272/full)
- Yoshioka, Bensmaïa, Craig & Hsiao 2007 (textures via finger vs. probe): probe-mediated texture perception is multidimensional (rough/hard/sticky); with a probe "roughness is not coded spatially, but instead is coded temporally by vibrations that are transmitted through the probe to receptors in the hand" (RA and PC afferents); correlations of log vibratory power with perceived roughness/hardness/stickiness were 0.92 / 0.04 / 0.23, of log compliance 0.43 / −0.93 / 0.59, and of log friction 0.57 / −0.54 / 0.82 — [Somatosens Mot Res 24(1–2) 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17558923); [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08990220701318163)
- Klatzky & Lederman: feeling textures through a probe depends on probe/surface geometry and exploratory speed/force — [Attention, Perception & Psychophysics](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03194587); Pacinian-based roughness constancy through tools — [J Neurosci 2011](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/31/48/17603)
- Surgical drill breakthrough (3 spine surgeons, 12 pigs, 10k–30k RPM): "the surgeon relies on the feel of the drill to determine whether the bone has been penetrated"; reaction time to penetration 0.226 ± 0.181 s (range 0.10–0.76 s) with overshoot 3.52 ± 1.77 mm; with automated haptic penetration detection 0.020 ± 0.012 s and 0.541 ± 0.351 mm; mean improvement "0.049 ± 0.019 s" (as reported) and "2.511 ± 0.537 mm" — [PMC9837054](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9837054/)
- Screwdriving haptics: "people apply axial force to the screws to avoid screwdriver slippage (cam-outs)"; axial force correlates with required torque and "type of the screw head influences the amount of axial force applied"; robot experiments "confirm the optimality of the strategy, used by humans"; cam-outs "correspond to sharp drops in torque" — [arXiv 1801.10386](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.10386)
- Human wrist torque-change JND has been reported around 12–19 % (relative force-change JND ~8–13 %) in haptics literature summarized in search results, but I could not confirm the primary source — treat as unverified (see Gaps) — [search-result summary only; see e.g. arXiv 2608.19635](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.19635)

### Inferences
- "Is the driver engaged with the screw?" is, for humans, a multi-cue judgement: sustained axial reaction force, torque build-up vs. sudden torque drop (cam-out/strip-out), and broadband vibration through the handle; the surgical numbers show that even experts need ~200 ms to react to an unexpected state change through a running power tool, so a robot should treat engagement detection as an anticipatory estimate updated by a high-rate force/vibration stream rather than a reactive trigger.
- Yoshioka's result that probe-mediated information is carried by vibration (PC/RA channels) means a robot hand needs high-bandwidth (≥ several hundred Hz) tactile/accelerometric sensing at the grip to "feel through" a hammer or drill; low-rate pressure arrays (e.g., 17 Hz gloves) cannot capture this.

### Gaps
- Direct human-factors studies of how *operators* detect drill bit engagement/stall or screw seating (cue weighting of sound vs. torque vs. vibration) were not found; the surgical-drill and screwdriving-haptics papers are the closest evidence. A Springer paper on virtual drilling perception with sound and kinesthetic cues exists but was paywalled — [J Multimodal User Interfaces 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12193-023-00407-8)
- Human torque-JND numbers above are from a search summary whose primary source I could not open.

## Part C-2. Tactile-glove and force-grounded human datasets (Meta, Tesla, Sharpa, EgoTactile, VTDexManip, Hoi!, OSMO, STAG, ActionSense, ForceMimic): what force/tactile channels exist, and does any cover impacts or power tools?

(F/T + tactile on handheld grippers), ForceMimic (6-axis F/T, peeling). **No public human dataset was found with tactile or force channels during hammering or power-tool use**; the only human impact-force recordings found are the sensorized stone-hammer strikes (135–730 N at 100 kHz) and ergonomics grip/feed-force measurements on hammer drills, neither packaged for learning.

### Cited Findings
- **EgoTactile (2026):** pressure glove with "M=162 sensors with 0–350 N range, sampled at ~17 Hz"; DJI Action 5 Pro 1280×720 at 30 fps (neck/head mount); 12 participants; "319k frames" (~5.82 h gloved); 63 objects in 7 categories including tools "Screwdriver", "TapeMeasure", "PaintBrush" (no hammers, drills, knives); tasks are natural grasp cycles without prescribed force profiles; no explicit hand-pose channel; no robot deployment yet — [arXiv 2606.09243](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09243)
- **VTDexManip (ICLR 2025):** human vision-tactile dataset "10 daily tasks and 182 objects", 2,032 sequences, 565k visual-tactile pairs from 5 subjects (per the search summary); tactile is "binary and sparse" yet adding it "boosts the success rate by about 20%" with a further 20 % from joint pretraining; benchmark tasks: BottleCap Turning, Faucet Screwing, Lever Sliding, Table Reorientation, In-hand Reorientation, Bimanual Hand-over — [ICLR 2025 abstract](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/e19b6f65791e350347bcff8a3955cb5b-Abstract-Conference.html); [GitHub](https://github.com/LQTS/VTDexManip)
- **STAG glove (Sundaram et al., Nature 2019):** 548 piezoresistive sensors on a knitted glove, 26 objects, 135,000 frames, ~$10 sensor array; used to identify objects and estimate weight from tactile maps — [Nature 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1234-z) (abstract via [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE%3A%22Learning%20the%20signatures%20of%20the%20human%20grasp%20using%20a%20scalable%20tactile%20glove%22&resultType=core&format=json&pageSize=1))
- **ActionSense (MIT, 2022):** eye tracking + first-person camera, forearm EMG, 17-IMU body tracking, finger-tracking gloves, "custom tactile sensors on hands with conductive thread matrices", 5 RGB + 1 depth cameras + 2 mics; activities include "peeling and slicing" with knives, spreading, jar opening, dishwasher loading; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 — [ActionSense](https://action-sense.csail.mit.edu/)
- **Hoi! (2025):** "3048 sequences across 381 articulated objects in 38 environments" across four embodiments (bare hand, hand + wrist camera, UMI gripper, custom Hoi! gripper); gripper embodiments provide "end-effector forces and tactile sensing"; CC BY 4.0 — [arXiv 2512.04884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04884)
- **OSMO open-source tactile glove (2025):** "12 three-axis tactile sensors across the fingertips and palm" (normal + shear); records tactile, hand pose (via hand tracking) and video; wearing OSMO on both human and robot "minimizes the visual and tactile embodiment gap"; wiping policy 72 % with no robot data — [arXiv 2512.08920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08920)
- **DexTouch-WM (Sept 2026):** "flexible piezoresistive arrays with a shared sensing layout on both human and dexterous robot hands"; action-conditioned world model predicting RGB + bilateral tactile; scaling human data from 0 to 100 h with 5 h robot data fixed improved held-out contact prediction — [arXiv 2609.20649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20649)
- **ForceMimic / ForceCapture (2024):** handheld device with "a six-axis force sensor placed between the end-effector and the user's gripping handle", ~$50 printed parts, 0.8 kg; zucchini peeling only (15 zucchinis, 438 segments, 30,199 action sequences); HybridIL switches between IK position and hybrid force-position primitives; 100 % success, 54.5 % relative improvement over vision-only; no hammering/drilling — [ForceMimic](https://forcemimic.github.io/)
- **Reconfigurable data glove (2023):** aggregates manipulation force via "customized force sensors made from soft and thin piezoresistive material" for physical and VR grasps — [arXiv 2301.05821](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.05821)
- **Tesla data collection:** 2024 job posts required "motion-capture suits and virtual reality headsets" and "sometimes ... haptic gloves"; $48/h, height 5'7"–5'11" — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/08/19/tesla-robot-hiring-workers-optimus-training-ai); [heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Tesla-hires-over-50-people-for-Optimus-training-in-motion-capture-suits-9841052.html); reported June 2025 shift to camera-only helmets with five cameras and 30–40 lb packs, workers "wiping tables, lifting cups, and pulling curtains" — [Longbridge](https://longbridge.com/en/news/263958780); the robot side gets a replaceable "sensing glove" due to touch-sensor reliability — [Electrek 2026](https://electrek.co/2026/09/25/tesla-optimus-production-ramp-hands-ai-generalization-problems/)

### Inferences
- The tactile-glove datasets sample at ≤ ~17–30 Hz (EgoTactile 17 Hz; most pose streams 30 Hz), far below the vibration bandwidth humans use to sense through tools (PC afferents; Part B-4) and below what impact events (<20 ms load rise) require. A hammering/drilling dataset would need ≥1 kHz force/accelerometer channels at the handle plus tactile at ≥100 Hz to be useful for the user's fast loop.
- The most practical near-term route is a ForceMimic/OSMO-style instrumented tool handle (6-axis F/T + IMU at the grip, shared layout on human and robot hand) rather than a full glove, since Part B-3 shows the loads are palm/power-grip dominated.

### Gaps
- "ManiVID" could not be identified as a dataset in any source reached (a similarly named "ManiVideo" is a hand-object video *generation* paper — [arXiv 2412.16212](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.16212)); Sharpa data gloves were not verifiable from primary sources.
- Sampling rates for STAG, OSMO and VTDexManip gloves and the exact VTDexManip glove taxel count were not in the reachable text (the ICLR PDF could not be parsed).
- No public dataset with tactile/force channels during hammering, drilling, or impact driving was found; this is a genuine gap rather than a search failure.
