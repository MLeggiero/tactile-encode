# Control, Planning, and RL for Dynamic, High-Energy Tool Use (Hammering, Drilling, Screwdriving, Impacts)

Scope note: notes cover ~2015–Sept 2026 with foundational earlier work. "Demonstrated" = real hardware; sim-only results are labeled. Where a paper's hardware/rates could not be verified from the primary source, this is stated. The web-search budget was exhausted mid-task; later coverage relied on direct fetches of arXiv abstracts/HTML, the arXiv API, and extracted PDFs, so some paywalled industrial-journal sources are cited from abstracts/search snippets only (flagged where relevant).

## Key Question 1: Impact-aware control — what did the EU I.AM. project (TU/e Saccon, Kober et al.) and related reference-spreading / post-impact work produce, and is there any hammering or "dynamic swing" result?

### Takeaway
The I.AM. project produced a mature, experimentally validated impact-aware control stack (reference spreading in a QP torque controller at 1 kHz on dual Franka arms, momentum-observer-based impact detection, nonsmooth-mechanics impact maps validated to ~3% post-impact-velocity error), but every hardware result is a logistics-style box hit/grab/toss at 0.3–0.6 m/s; no I.AM. or reference-spreading paper demonstrates hammering, tool swinging, or repeated percussive impacts, and none uses tactile sensing or a learned policy.

### Cited Findings
- I.AM. is an H2020 project (GA 871899) on "impact aware manipulation in logistics" using torque-controlled robots to "exploit intentional impacts for manipulation"; partners are TU Eindhoven, EPFL, TU Munich, CNRS, Algoryx, Franka Robotics, Smart Robotics and Vanderlande — [I.AM. project site](https://i-am-project.eu/)
- The project's results page lists 40+ publications (2020–2025) on post-impact velocity prediction, impact-aware MPC, collision detection, soft-contact modeling with deformable end-effector pads, dual-arm grabbing/tossing with simultaneous impacts, and use cases named TOSS, BOX and GRAB; the Franka Emika robot is the reference platform; no hammering, tool use or dynamic swinging appears — [I.AM. results page](https://i-am-project.eu/index.php/results/)
- Reference spreading (RS) is "a hybrid tracking control approach that deals with impacts by defining ante- and post-impact references that are coupled by an impact map at the nominal impact time and overlap around this time"; it was introduced by Saccon et al. (2014) — [van Steen et al., arXiv 2305.08643](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.08643)
- van Steen, van den Brandt, van de Wouw, Kober, Saccon, "Quadratic Programming-based Reference Spreading Control for Dual-Arm Robotic Manipulation with Planned Simultaneous Impacts," IEEE T-RO 2024: two 7-DOF Franka Emika arms with custom compliant silicone end-effectors; sensing = joint encoders plus a momentum observer for external-force estimation (no dedicated F/T sensors), with four OptiTrack Prime x22 cameras used only to validate object position; control loop 1 kHz (Δt = 0.001 s); impact detection uses a 200 ms observation window; objects 0.64–2.12 kg; ±30 mm object displacement to force non-simultaneous impacts; 600 experiments; grasp success 90% (proposed) vs 85% (no interim mode), 80% (no RS), 40% (no velocity feedback); lowest impact-induced torque peaks vs all baselines; this is "the first experimental evaluation of RS control on a robotic setup"; references were generated by teleoperation; future work = a contact-completion detector and autonomous impact-aware planners — [arXiv 2305.08643 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.08643)
- van Steen, van de Wouw, Saccon, "Impact-Aware Control using Time-Invariant Reference Spreading" (submitted to T-RO, Nov 2024): dual 7-DOF Franka arms with silicone end-effectors at 1 kHz; no vision — object position "inferred from the state of the robot(s), assuming the object and the robot are in contact"; tasks = single-arm hit-and-push and dual-arm grabbing of a 0.60 kg parcel, 1.30 kg cat-food box and 2.20 kg juice pack; 600 experiments (300 per task); proposed method gives the lowest averaged maximum desired acceleration norm vs three baselines; limitations: the interim (partial-contact) mode "lasts for a user-defined time … may require manual tuning for different types of object," no formal stability proof, extension to humanoids open — [arXiv 2411.09870 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.09870v1)
- van Steen, Stokbroekx, van de Wouw, Saccon, "Impact-Aware Robotic Manipulation: Quantifying the Sim-To-Real Gap for Velocity Jumps" (2024): Franka dual-arm with silicone-covered end-effectors; 4 kHz low-level torque loop; simulator = AGX Dynamics (nonsmooth time-stepping, chosen because it "doesn't require contact stiffness/damping parameter estimation"); post-impact velocities 0.33–0.59 m/s (hit-and-push) and 0.32–0.52 m/s (grabbing); "3.1% average error between the post-impact velocity identified from simulations and from experiments"; key caveat: feedback control acts while impact-induced vibrations are still active, so evaluating impacts from raw velocity signals is unreliable — the RS framework is what makes the comparison meaningful — [arXiv 2411.06319 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06319v1)
- Aouaj, Padois, Saccon, "Predicting the Post-Impact Velocity of a Robotic Arm via Rigid Multibody Models: an Experimental Study," ICRA 2021: 7-DOF torque-controlled arm, 18 impact experiments against rigid surfaces; impact map from nonsmooth mechanics (frictionless, inelastic); prediction is "promising" but the rigid model "presents no oscillations" whereas real data show "damped oscillatory response after the impact" — [arXiv 2010.08220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08220)
- Lineage of RS papers (arXiv API sweep): 2111.05211 (van Steen 2021, simultaneous-impact QP-RS, simulation on rigid and flexible-joint models only), 2212.00877 (van Steen 2022, dual-arm impact-aware grasping, simulation only with flexible joints and low-level torque control), 2305.08643 and 2411.09870 (hardware, above), 2405.02184 (Bertollo 2024, RS for bipedal walking, simulation). None mentions hammering, swinging or tools — [arXiv API listing](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=abs:%22reference%20spreading%22&max_results=20&sortBy=submittedDate&sortOrder=descending)
- Stouraitis, Yan, Moura, Gienger, Vijayakumar, "Multi-mode Trajectory Optimization for Impact-aware Manipulation," IROS 2020: incorporates "an explicit contact force transmission model" (second-order critically damped) into TO so that contact forces, contact timings, motion and compliance are optimized together; hardware = KUKA LWR at 200 Hz, Vicon at 100 Hz for object tracking, ATI F/T sensor sampled at 100 Hz (measurement only); a 20 kg object travelling at ~0.66 m/s (0.88 m/s in Fig. 1) is halted with maximum force < 20 N (full workspace) / < 30 N (reduced workspace) vs a soft-compliance baseline; simulations in Bullet — [Edinburgh PDF](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/svijayak/publications/stouraitis-IROS2020.pdf); [arXiv 2006.13374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.13374)
- Yan, Stouraitis, Moura, Xu, Gienger, Vijayakumar, "Impact-Aware Bimanual Catching of Large-Momentum Objects" (2024): multi-mode TO solved by SQP optimizing "end-effector motion, stiffness, and contact force," executed through an indirect force controller on a compliant bimanual system; real-world catching of objects with well-defined, constrained and free-flying motion — [arXiv 2403.17249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17249)
- Tassi et al., "IMA-Catcher: An IMpact-Aware Nonprehensile Catching Framework based on Combined Optimization and Learning," IJRR 2025: position/velocity/stiffness trajectories learned from human demonstrations of catching free-falling objects; hierarchical QP controller minimizes reflected mass at the end-effector; "without velocity matching, [the] task would be infeasible due to excessive joint torques" — [arXiv 2506.20801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20801)
- Kirner & Ott, "The Nonsmooth Impact Direction (NSID) of Robotic Systems" (July 2026, under review): defines a characteristic impact direction "largely independent of contact properties," with experimental validation, and explicitly names "repetitive hammering" as a future application for impact-aware planning/control — [arXiv 2607.13768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13768)
- Other impact-aware controllers in the sweep are simulation-only: Wang et al. 2020 humanoid QP whole-body controller allowing non-zero contact velocity (arXiv 2001.08454), Gao et al. 2019 impact-aware bipedal planner (arXiv 1910.10633), Li et al. 2020 hybrid-systems DDP (arXiv 2006.08102) — [arXiv API listing](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=abs:%22impact-aware%22%20AND%20(abs:manipulation%20OR%20abs:robot)&max_results=30&sortBy=submittedDate&sortOrder=descending)
- The one impact-aware paper that hammers a nail, Ti, Gao, Zhao, Calinon, "An Optimal Control Formulation of Tool Affordance Applied to Impact Tasks" (2024), is model-based (iLQR + ADMM with a directional-velocity-manipulability cost) rather than RS; details are in Key Question 2 — [arXiv 2402.05502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05502)

### Inferences
- The RS stack is the most rigorous existing treatment of "what happens to a torque-controlled arm at the instant of a planned impact," and its key engineering ideas (overlapping ante/post-impact references, an interim mode for partial contact, momentum-observer impact detection, no F/T sensor needed) transfer directly to hammer strikes — but they were only validated for single impacts at ≤0.6 m/s with compliant pads, not for repeated high-velocity strikes through a hand-held tool.
- The Aouaj/van Steen finding that post-impact vibration corrupts velocity feedback for tens of ms is directly relevant to a high-frequency grip/torque regulator: any learned or model-based post-impact controller must be designed to tolerate (or exploit) the ringing rather than trust raw joint-velocity/effort signals immediately after contact.
- Stouraitis/Yan show that jointly optimizing stiffness with motion is what makes 20 kg-at-0.9 m/s impacts survivable at <30 N; the analogue for hammering is scheduling grip/arm impedance across the swing, strike and rebound phases.

### Gaps
- No I.AM./RS paper reports impact velocities above ~0.6 m/s or impacts through a grasped tool; whether RS stays stable when the impacting body is a hand-held object with its own grasp compliance is unaddressed.
- I.AM. deliverables on the TOSS use case and impact-aware MPC were not individually fetched (results page only summarized), so control rates and hardware for those sub-results are not recorded here.
- NSID (Kirner & Ott 2026) full experimental details (robot, rates, whether any hammering experiment exists) could not be extracted from the abstract.

## Key Question 2: Hammering / nailing — which papers demonstrate a robot hammering a nail on real hardware, and what grip-force / regrasp handling do they include?

### Takeaway
Real-hardware nail hammering exists in three flavors — model-based optimal control (Ti/Calinon 2024, Franka), vision-only learned policies (TOG-Net 2018/2020, Tool-as-Interface 2025, PoCo 2024), and an RL grip-adaptation controller on a dexterous hand (Grasp-to-Act 2026) — but none regulates grip force from tactile sensing during the strike, none closes a force loop on the impact itself, and the planning line (Holladay, Toussaint) either handles quasi-static nail pulling with a fixed grasp or stays in simulation.

### Cited Findings

#### Model-based / planning
- Holladay, Lozano-Pérez, Rodriguez, "Force-and-Motion Constrained Planning for Tool Use," IROS 2019: hardware = ABB YuMi "with custom printed fingers that allowed us to control the radius and coefficient of friction"; each part-rig sits on an ATI Gamma F/T sensor whose "readings … are not used in-the-loop of the task" (analysis only); tasks = hammer (claw) nail pulling, screw driving, wrench turning, and knife cutting; friction modeled with planar limit-surface models; the planner "currently fix[es] a grasp throughout the duration of the task" and constrains each task "to be completed in one continuous motion" — regrasping and "stop-readjust-continue strategies" are listed as future work; a printable tool benchmark is released — [IROS 2019 PDF](https://lis.csail.mit.edu/pubs/holladay-iros19.pdf); [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8967889/)
- Holladay, Lozano-Pérez, Rodriguez, "Robust Planning for Multi-stage Forceful Manipulation," IJRR (2023/2024): augments a TAMP planner with a "forceful kinematic chain constraint" capturing torque and frictional limits; domains = childproof bottle (push-and-twist), nut twisting, vegetable cutting; cost-sensitive planning finds strategies robust to uncertainty in physical parameters; no hammering/impact task — [arXiv 2208.00319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00319); [IJRR](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/02783649231198560)
- Toussaint, Allen, Smith, Tenenbaum, "Differentiable Physics and Stable Modes for Tool-Use and Manipulation Planning," RSS 2018 (IJCAI 2019 extended abstract): TAMP with primitives that "either impose stable kinematic constraints or differentiable dynamical and impulse exchange constraints at the path optimization level," demonstrated on "physical puzzles that involve tool use and dynamic interactions" (hitting, throwing) — [IJCAI 2019 abstract](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2019/0869.pdf). Real-hardware evaluation could not be confirmed from accessible sources (see Gaps); treat as simulation-only.
- Ti, Gao, Zhao, Calinon (Idiap/HIT), "An Optimal Control Formulation of Tool Affordance Applied to Impact Tasks" (2024): iLQR + ADMM with a directional velocity-manipulability cost; real robot = 7-axis Franka Emika under joint-velocity commands (limits 2.175 rad/s joints 1–4, 2.610 rad/s joints 5–7); two-finger gripper with "two pieces of sponge on the two grippers" to mimic the soft hand-hammer connection; 3D-printed ABS hammer with cylindrical handle, allowed grasp region 50–230 mm from the head; foam pegboard with pre-drilled pilot holes; no force sensor ("an external force sensor … mounted on the head of the hammer … would increase the weight … and cause a bias for the gravity center, further increasing the grip instability") — impact force inferred from nail insertion depth via quadratic regression on 15 samples per tightness level; 12 nail locations; insertion depths 45–80 mm (slack) and 35–75 mm (tight) with the directional metric; limitations: "the held hammer can sometimes be rotated or shifted" in the gripper, no regrasp or active grip-force control (tool treated as a fixed link), collision avoidance not considered — [arXiv 2402.05502 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.05502)

#### Learned policies on real hardware
- Fang, Zhu, Garg, Kurenkov, Mehta, Fei-Fei, Savarese, "Learning Task-Oriented Grasping for Tool Manipulation from Simulated Self-Supervision," RSS 2018 / IJRR 2020 (TOG-Net): 7-DOF Sawyer with parallel-jaw gripper and an overhead Kinect2 (depth image is the only observation); models "solely trained using simulated data" (Bullet, procedurally generated tools); control is "open-loop … using parameterized motion primitives," each a planar trajectory whose parameters (ax, ay, az, af) the policy predicts; real-world success 80.0% hammering and 71.1% sweeping with 9 unseen objects; failures "usually caused by occasional deviations during the execution of grasping or manipulation"; no force/tactile sensing, no grip-force control — [arXiv 1806.09266](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.09266); [IJRR](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0278364919872545)
- Chen, Zhu, Liu, Li, Driggs-Campbell, "Tool-as-Interface: Learning Robot Policies from Observing Human Tool Use," CoRL 2025: robots = Kinova Gen3 and UR5e; RealSense D415 RGB only; diffusion policy mapping single-view RGB to SE(3) tool-centric task-space actions, tool pose tracked by FoundationPose; nail hammering = "locate the nail, draw back the hammer, and strike the nail tip accurately" (nail < 15.5 mm); 13/13 successes vs 0/13 for teleop diffusion policy and 0/13 for UMI with 25 demos (13/13 with 100 UMI demos); overall 71% higher success and 77% less collection time than teleoperation; "no force/tactile feedback or grip force control"; assumes rigid tool attachment — "minor shifts may occur" in contact-rich tasks — [arXiv 2504.04612 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04612)
- Wang, Zhao, Du, Adelson, Tedrake, "PoCo: Policy Composition from and for Heterogeneous Robot Learning," RSS 2024: Franka Panda with a "simplified version of the GelSight Svelte Hand" (one activated GelSight finger), overhead RealSense D435 and wrist D405; modalities RGB, point cloud and tactile; hammering task = "pressing a pin downward by 2 centimeters using a hammer tool"; policies run at 10 Hz (RGB) or 6 Hz (point cloud), composition at 5 Hz; end-effector velocity actions; authors note policies "can overfit and perform poorly in tasks requiring delicate contact control"; ~20% improvement over baselines — [arXiv 2402.02511 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02511v3); [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2024/technique-for-more-effective-multipurpose-robots-0603)
- Gupta, Mirzaee, Yuan, "Grasp to Act: Dexterous Grasping for Tool Use in Dynamic Settings," RA-L 2026 (vol. 11 no. 5): 16-DoF LEAP hand on a UR5, RealSense D435 plus OptiTrack for object tracking; an RL grasp-adaptation policy uses proprioception only — "the policy infers the object's state indirectly through hand joint positions, targets, and torques" — with no tactile sensors; adaptation and wrist trajectory run at 30 Hz; the arm replays the human-demonstrated object trajectory open-loop; hammering task = "Drive a nail 2 cm above a drywall stack using a steel claw hammer … with 4 strikes," with real impacts; results 100% success, translational slip 0.69 cm and rotational slip 1.35° vs 0.93 cm / 4.07° for the RL base; zero-shot sim-to-real across hammering, sawing, cutting, stirring, scooping; limitations: assumes unobstructed initial grasp region and "relatively simple" geometries — [arXiv 2602.20466 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20466); [arXiv API record](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=ti:%22Grasp%20to%20Act%22%20AND%20ti:dexterous&max_results=5)
- Jangir, Hansen, Ghosal, Jain, Wang, "Look Closer: Bridging Egocentric and Third-Person Views with Transformers for Robotic Manipulation," RA-L 2022: RL with cross-view attention between a static third-person camera and a wrist egocentric camera; sim-to-real with uncalibrated cameras and no state information; "hammer manipulation task … succeeds in 75% of trials" vs 38% (multi-view) and 13% (single-view) baselines; robot model not stated in abstract — [arXiv 2201.07779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07779)
- Zorina, Carpentier, Sivic, Petrík, "Learning to Manipulate Tools by Aligning Simulation to Video Demonstration," RA-L 2021: tools = spade, scythe, hammer learned from YouTube videos via trajectory alignment + RL/optimization; real-world validation on a Franka Emika Panda covered the spade only; hammer results are simulation — [arXiv 2111.03088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03088)
- Liang et al., "DexHandDiff: Interaction-aware Diffusion Planning for Adaptive Dexterous Manipulation," CVPR 2025: tasks include "hammer striking"; 59.2% vs 29.5% average success on goal-adaptive tasks; hardware unspecified in abstract (benchmark tasks match the Adroit suite; treat hammer result as sim unless verified) — [arXiv 2411.18562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18562)
- Kim, Kim, Lee, Jang, Kim, "ARMADA: A low-cost and lightweight 6 DoF bimanual arm for dynamic and contact-rich manipulation" (2025): $6,100 bimanual arm with low-inertia back-drivable actuators, 6.16 m/s end-effector speed, 2.5 kg payload, no torque sensors; "dynamic manipulation like snatching, hammering, and bimanual throwing in real-world environments" plus a zero-shot sim-to-real nonprehensile policy; abstract does not say whether hammering is learned or whether a nail is driven — [arXiv 2502.16908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16908)
- Liu & Li, "Learning Slope-Adaptive Whole-Body Locomotion for Humanoid Robots in Roofing Construction" (Sept 2026): Unitree G1; RL policy at 50 Hz over 200 Hz PD (decimation 4), torso depth camera; "hammering" and "nailgun" tasks are hand-trajectory/posture reproduction only — "no actual nails are driven and no real impacts occur," "no force or tactile feedback," "tools are not actuated," hoist-assisted; work-clearance errors 0.256–0.531 cm and 3/3 per task — [arXiv 2609.20558 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20558)
- Pang et al., "A Direct Rigid Transmission 2-DoF Wrist Extension for Tendon-Driven Hand" (Sept 2026): servo-driven wrist mechanism demonstrated on real hardware performing "nail hammering" among other tasks (mechanism paper, no learning) — [arXiv 2609.22681](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22681)
- Phys2Real (Wang et al. 2025) evaluates hammer *pushing* (planar, off-center mass) with online parameter adaptation, not striking; DORA (Zhang et al. 2025) includes a hammer-use task with a multi-fingered hand that "appears simulation-based" — [arXiv API sweep](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=abs:hammer%20AND%20abs:robot%20AND%20(abs:%22reinforcement%20learning%22%20OR%20abs:tactile%20OR%20abs:diffusion%20OR%20abs:humanoid)&max_results=30&sortBy=submittedDate&sortOrder=descending)

#### Industrial / humanoid demos and sim benchmarks
- Torobo (Tokyo Robotics, July 2024 press): humanoid (1,660 mm, 680 mm reach; 7-axis dual arms, 3-axis waist, omni base) with "torque sensors at all of the joints of its arms and waist"; joint-torque control with impedance control; "the rebound force from the hammer is absorbed through a combination of rubber material elasticity, deflection in torque sensors and harmonic gears, backdrivability, and impedance control, which allows the nail to be driven with a certain amount of force"; positioned for future ML research — no evidence the hammering motion is learned — [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/innovation/torobo-humanoid-robot-hammers-nail); [Malay Mail, 25 Jul 2024](https://www.malaymail.com/news/tech-gadgets/2024/07/25/new-japanese-humanoid-robot-performs-diy-tasks-including-hammering-nails-and-sawing-wood-video/144918)
- Adroit "hammer" (Rajeswaran et al. 2017, DAPG) is the canonical sim-only RL hammering benchmark: MuJoCo, 28-DoF (24-DoF ShadowHand + 4-DoF arm), "pick up a hammer and drive a nail into a board," success when "the entire length of the nail is inside the board," nail resists up to 15 N of dry friction — [Gymnasium-Robotics docs](https://robotics.farama.org/envs/adroit_hand/adroit_hammer/)
- A 2015 ResearchGate entry "Hitting the Nail on the Head: An Investigation of Robot Learning" describes a robot arm driving nails into a board to evaluate controllers that "learned to improve their efficiency at driving nails as they drove more nails" — page could not be fetched (403); authors/venue unverified — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/275584670_Hitting_the_Nail_on_the_Head_An_Investigation_of_Robot_Learning)

### Inferences
- Across all real-hardware hammering demos, the hammer is either treated as a rigid extension of the gripper (TOG-Net, Tool-as-Interface, Ti/Calinon, Torobo) or slip is fought with proprioception-only RL finger corrections (Grasp-to-Act); no system measures tool–hand slip or strike force with tactile/F-T sensing in the loop. The gap the user's project targets (tactile-driven grip/torque regulation during the strike) is genuinely open.
- Ti/Calinon's explicit refusal to mount a force sensor on the hammer, and their observed in-gripper hammer rotation, quantify the exact failure mode (grip instability under impact) that tactile grip regulation would address.
- Grasp-to-Act is the only work that adapts the grasp *during* strikes, but at 30 Hz from proprioception with an open-loop arm; it is a low-level regulator, not a learned high-level policy, and does not sense the target (nail) state.
- Learned high-level hammering policies run at 5–10 Hz (PoCo, Tool-as-Interface) and rely on vision alone, so they cannot react to a mis-hit within a strike; they are natural "slow" layers for the user's architecture.

### Gaps
- Toussaint et al. RSS 2018: could not access full text (PDF endpoints returned binary; Semantic Scholar page empty) to confirm whether any real-robot demonstration exists.
- Levine, Finn, Darrell, Abbeel (JMLR 2016) real tasks: only "screwing a cap onto a bottle" was confirmed from the JMLR page; the frequently cited "hammer claw under a nail" PR2 task could not be verified here — [JMLR page](https://jmlr.org/papers/v17/15-522.html)
- Fang et al.: the exact real-world hammering success criterion (what is struck, depth) was not extracted from the IJRR text.
- Look Closer, DexHandDiff, ARMADA: robot models, sensing details and whether real nails were driven are not stated in abstracts.
- No paper found that reports per-strike impact force/velocity on a real robot with a hand-held hammer other than the indirect insertion-depth proxy of Ti et al.

## Key Question 3: Drilling and screwdriving — force/torque-based and learned control, and which signals detect engagement, cam-out or bit slip?

### Takeaway
Screwdriving research splits into (i) industrial/disassembly work where engagement and cam-out are detected from F/T or joint-torque signatures (sharp torque drops, axial-force/torque proportionality) and (ii) 2024–2026 dexterous-hand work that turns screwdrivers with sim-trained RL plus tactile BC or online parameter estimation; robotic drilling is dominated by manufacturing papers on low-stiffness chatter and force control, with only isolated learned drilling-phase classifiers (from force) and one tactile-hand demo of activating a power drill; no work was found that closes a learned high-frequency loop on drill/screw engagement.

### Cited Findings

#### Screwdriving with hand tools / dexterous hands
- Tang, Jia, Xue, "Robotic Manipulation of Hand Tools: The Case of Screwdriving," ICRA 2024 (Iowa State): Shadow Dexterous Hand on a Barrett 4-DOF WAM; screwdriver pose from an Azure Kinect + ArUco markers, refined by optimization under visual and finger-contact constraints; "Since the Shadow Hand was not equipped with tactile sensors," fingertip forces are estimated by mapping strain-gauge readings to joint torques and running forward dynamics; the hand "accepts position commands only," so the dynamics-based force controller is adapted to generate position targets; the wrench on the screw is estimated from screwdriver dynamics and mapped through the grasp matrix to fingertip forces (backward chaining), with rolling contacts and finger gaits; simulation in MuJoCo, which "cannot simulate the complex interactions between the screw and the substrate" — replaced by a 1-DOF screw with rising resistance torque; 20 real trials, 17 exceeding five rounds of rolling/gaiting; failures when "the screwdriver's handle slipped off the fingers or its tip slid out of the screw's drive," attributed to contact-update errors, force-estimate errors and "vibrations of the Shadow Hand" — [ICRA 2024 PDF](https://www.cs.iastate.edu/files/inline-files/icra24.pdf); [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10610831/)
- Hsieh, Hsieh, Wang, Lin, Malik, Sreenath, Qi, "Learning Dexterous Manipulation Skills from Imperfect Simulations," ICRA 2026: three stages — RL in simulation with simplified object models, using the policy as a skill primitive inside teleoperation to collect real demos, then behavior cloning "that incorporates tactile sensing"; tasks = nut-bolt fastening and screwdriving on multi-fingered hands; motivated by "the difficulty of simulating complex contact dynamics and multisensory signals, especially tactile feedback"; reports "high task progress ratios compared to direct sim-to-real transfer" — [arXiv 2512.02011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02011)
- Kumar, Iba, Soltani Zarrin, Berenson, "PLUME: Probabilistic Latent Unified World Modeling and Parameter Estimation for Multi-Finger Manipulation" (2026): world model that maintains a belief over object shape, pose and friction online; zero-shot sim-to-real on a hardware screwdriver-turning task, beating offline-RL and world-model-augmented BC baselines; notes "standard domain randomization strategies may be insufficient for precise tasks" — [arXiv 2606.11396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11396)
- Feng et al., "A Minimalist Retargeting-Guided Reinforcement Learning Recipe for Dexterous Manipulation" (2026): single human demo retargeted plus residual RL tracking object keypoints; zero-shot hardware transfer on two multi-fingered hands for scissors and screwdriver turning "with careful system identification" — [arXiv 2607.11874](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11874)
- Related multi-finger screwdriver-turning hardware work from the same sweep: AVO (Hung 2025, learned value functions guiding TO for contact-mode switching), diffusion-based recovery for screwdriver turning (Kumar 2025, "improves task recovery by 96%"), diffusion-informed contact search (Kumar 2024), differentiable rolling-constraint TO (Yang 2024), DexterityGen (Yin 2025, RL primitives + teleop prompts for screwdrivers) — [arXiv API sweep](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=abs:screwdriving%20OR%20abs:%22screw%20fastening%22%20OR%20abs:%22screw%20driving%22&max_results=25&sortBy=submittedDate&sortOrder=descending)
- Oller, Berenson, Fazeli, "Tactile-Driven Non-Prehensile Object Manipulation via Extrinsic Contact Mode Control" (2024): high-resolution compliant tactile sensors on a gripper; frames "screwdriver tightening a screw" as an instance of extrinsic contact-mode control; demonstrates planar sliding/pivoting building blocks (no full screwdriving result stated in abstract) — [arXiv 2405.18214](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18214)
- Hu, Wan, Koyama, Harada, "A Mechanical Screwing Tool for 2-Finger Parallel Grippers," T-RO 2021: scissor-like element + double ratchet converts repeated gripper linear motion into continuous rotation; elastic elements hold the tool and supply torque; real-world demonstrations with vision — [arXiv 2006.10366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10366)

## Key Question 4: Dynamic-manipulation foundations — Mason & Lynch, TossingBot, impedance/admittance tuned for impacts, learned variable impedance

### Takeaway
The foundational line (Lynch–Mason nonprehensile dynamics, TossingBot's residual physics, Buchli/Kalakrishnan PI² impedance learning, Bogdanovic's joint-space impedance action space, Martín-Martín's VICES) establishes that (a) exploiting dynamics requires planning through free-flight/impact modes and (b) learning *impedance* rather than torque is what makes RL robust to contact uncertainty — but none of these works targets impacts delivered through a hand-held tool, and the learned-impedance results are for wiping/insertion-type continuous contact, not percussive events.

### Cited Findings
- Lynch & Mason, "Dynamic Nonprehensile Manipulation: Controllability, Planning, and Experiments," IJRR 1999: low-DOF robots exploit "gravitational, centrifugal, and Coriolis forces as virtual motors" to control extra part DOF via rolling, slipping and free flight — [IJRR](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/027836499901800105); Lynch's 1996 thesis-era report — [CMU PDF](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/pub_files/pub2/lynch_kevin_1996_2/lynch_kevin_1996_2.pdf); Mason, "Progress in Nonprehensile Manipulation," IJRR 1999 — [IJRR](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/02783649922067762); impulsive manipulation (Huang, Krotkov, Mason 1995) and the Ruggiero–Lippiello–Siciliano nonprehensile dynamic manipulation survey — [survey PDF](https://www.iris.unina.it/retrieve/e268a72f-3a00-4c8f-e053-1705fe0a812c/J11.pdf)
- Zeng, Song, Lee, Rodriguez, Funkhouser, "TossingBot: Learning to Throw Arbitrary Objects with Residual Physics," RSS 2019: deep networks "predict residuals on top of control parameters predicted by a physics simulator"; 500+ mean picks per hour, 85% throwing accuracy; limitations include "acquiring reliable pre-throw conditions" and unmodeled mass distribution, friction, aerodynamics — [arXiv 1903.11239](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.11239)
- Buchli, Stulp, Theodorou, Schaal, "Learning variable impedance control," IJRR 30(7):820–833, 2011 (PI² learning of DMP parameters and time-varying gains) — [IJRR](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0278364911402527); RSS 2010 precursor — [RSS 2010 PDF](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss06/p20.pdf)
- Bogdanovic, Khadiv, Righetti, "Learning Variable Impedance Control for Contact Sensitive Tasks," RA-L 5(4):6129–6136, 2020: policy outputs joint-space impedance and desired position; compared against torque and position action spaces "under different contact uncertainties"; a regularizing reward term gives interpretability and eases transfer; results include running learned policies on a real system; direct torque output "has difficulty to robustly solve the problem without any given structure in the action space" — [arXiv 1907.07500](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.07500)
- Martín-Martín, Lee, Gardner, Savarese, Bohg, Garg, "Variable Impedance Control in End-Effector Space (VICES)," IROS 2019: end-effector impedance as RL action space on path following, door opening and surface wiping; improves sample efficiency, keeps low energy, ensures safety; policies transfer across robots in sim and sim-to-real — [arXiv 1906.08880](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08880)
- A 2020 review of variable impedance control and learning — [arXiv 2010.06246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06246)
- Impact-specific impedance/stiffness scheduling on hardware: Stouraitis 2020 co-optimizes stiffness with contact timing to halt 20 kg at ~0.9 m/s under 30 N — [Edinburgh PDF](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/svijayak/publications/stouraitis-IROS2020.pdf); IMA-Catcher learns stiffness trajectories from humans and minimizes reflected end-effector mass — [arXiv 2506.20801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20801); Torobo absorbs hammer rebound with joint-torque-sensor/harmonic-gear compliance plus impedance control — [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/innovation/torobo-humanoid-robot-hammers-nail)
- Taunyazov et al., "Extended Tactile Perception: Vibration Sensing through Tools and Grasped Objects" (2021): dynamic tactile sensors (BioTac micro-vibration, NUSkin) detect vibrations transmitted through held rigid objects; tasks = impact localization on a rod and food identification with a fork, on real hardware — [arXiv 2106.00489](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00489)

### Inferences
- The impedance-as-action-space results (Bogdanovic, VICES) are the closest existing answer to "how should a learned policy command a high-frequency controller for contact": output stiffness/damping + setpoint rather than torque. For hammering, the natural extension is a policy that schedules grip and arm impedance per swing phase, which no cited work has done.
- Taunyazov's vibration-through-tool sensing is the one demonstrated sensing primitive for "feeling" events at the tool tip through the grasp; it has not been combined with any impact controller.

### Gaps
- Kalakrishnan, Righetti, Pastor, Schaal, "Learning force control policies for compliant manipulation," IROS 2011, surfaced only in search snippets; no primary URL was retrieved.
- Classical hybrid force/position control (Raibert & Craig 1981) and impact-tuned admittance design references were not fetched within the budget.
- Bogdanovic's real-robot platform and control rates were not stated in the abstract.

## Key Question 5: Model-based RL / trajectory optimization with contact (MJPC, TD-MPC2, contact-implicit TO) — any tool-impact tasks?

### Takeaway
Contact-implicit trajectory optimization (Posa–Tedrake MPCC; hydroelastic iLQR in Drake) and sampling-based MPC (MJPC, MJX-parallel MPC on a Franka) plan through impacts in principle, and TD-MPC2 learns world models over hundreds of sim tasks, but none of the retrieved works targets a tool-mediated impact task; the 2026 hardware sampling-MPC result specifically warns that contact sensitivity and weak real-time adaptation signals limit contact-rich deployment.

### Cited Findings
- Posa, Cantu, Tedrake, "A direct method for trajectory optimization of rigid bodies through contact," IJRR 2014: plans through "inelastic impacts and Coulomb friction" by posing an MPCC, eliminating a priori mode ordering — [IJRR](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0278364913506757); background chapter — [Underactuated Robotics, contact chapter](https://underactuated.mit.edu/contact.html)
- Kurtz & Lin, "Contact-Implicit Trajectory Optimization with Hydroelastic Contact and iLQR," IROS 2022: Drake's pressure-field hydroelastic contact + iLQR; open-loop playback on a Kinova Gen3 for whole-arm manipulation; authors call results "preliminary" — [arXiv 2202.13986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13986)
- Recent CITO solvers (IMPACT augmented-Lagrangian active-set, 2026; ContactIPM interior-point, 2026) continue the line; no tool-impact task was verified for them — [arXiv 2605.09127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09127); [arXiv 2608.11731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11731)
- Howell, Gileadi, Tunyasuvunakool, Zakka, Erez, Tassa, "Predictive Sampling: Real-time Behaviour Synthesis with MuJoCo" (MJPC, 2022): open-source interactive predictive-control framework with iLQG, gradient descent and derivative-free predictive sampling; "This work does not present algorithmic advances"; no real-hardware deployment in the paper — [arXiv 2212.00541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00541)
- Dierking, Carvalho, Le, Chalvatzaki, Peters, "Real-World Deployment of Massively Parallel Sampling-Based MPC for Contact-Rich Manipulation" (ICRA 2026 workshop): Franka Research 3, MuJoCo MJX, Push-T, real-to-sim-to-real pipeline; structured global sampling (MTP) beats CEM, MPPI and predictive sampling on mode-switching tasks; challenges = "contact sensitivity, tight compute budgets, and the difficulty of obtaining informative domain-randomization signals in real time"; "contact-initiation parameters yield interpretable adaptation signals, whereas global physics parameters provide feedback that is too weak for reliable exploitation" — [arXiv 2606.20712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20712); related risk-aware DR for sampling MPC — [arXiv 2605.03290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03290)
- Hansen, Su, Wang, "TD-MPC2: Scalable, Robust World Models for Continuous Control," ICLR 2024: 104 online RL tasks across 4 domains; a single 317M-parameter agent for 80 tasks; no real-robot experiments stated — [arXiv 2310.16828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828)
- Gao, Liu, Wu, Chen, Du, "IMPACT: Learning Internal-Model Predictive Control for Forceful Robotic Manipulation" (June 2026): learned internal model for "using tools of varying weights, transporting objects with different masses, … table wiping," reducing reliance on wrist F/T or tactile sensors; sim + real; better generalization to unseen weights; no hammering/drilling — [arXiv 2606.10818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10818)
- Liu, Chen, Jang, Xu, Kira, Zhao, "Opt2VLA: Force-Aware Vision-Language-Action for Contact-Rich Humanoid Whole-Body Manipulation" (Sept 2026): Agility Digit (≈48 kg, 30 DoF); VLA at 5 Hz → force-aware RL whole-body controller at 200 Hz → PD at 1 kHz (sim) / 2 kHz (hardware); references from whole-body TO in Crocoddyl/Pinocchio with prescribed contact wrenches; force sensors (Bertec plate, SensX 160) used only for evaluation; tasks = surface wiping, shelf-box pushing, 0.5–2.0 kg box pickup; hardware force-tracking MAE 7.0 / 3.9 / 1.7 N; no tool tasks — [arXiv 2609.23968 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23968)

### Inferences
- No retrieved TO/MPC/world-model paper optimizes a percussive tool trajectory on hardware; MJPC-style sampling MPC would in principle plan a swing, but Dierking's hardware findings (contact sensitivity, weak DR signals) suggest impact timing/velocity would be the hardest quantities to adapt online.
- Opt2VLA is the closest architectural template to the user's proposal (slow VLA + 200 Hz force-aware RL controller + kHz PD) but its force references come from offline TO and its tasks are quasi-static.

### Gaps

- MJPC's task list (GitHub) was not fetched; no evidence either way of an impact/tool task.

## Key Question 6: Sim-to-real for high-frequency contact — known limitations of MuJoCo / Isaac / Drake / Genesis for impacts, vibration and tool–object coupling, and how papers work around them

### Takeaway
Hardware studies converge on three facts: rigid impact maps from nonsmooth engines predict post-impact *velocity jumps* well (≈3% error) but not the post-impact ringing; simulators cannot represent screw–substrate, articulated-tool joint friction/stiction, or racket-rubber contact without task-specific fixes; and the effective workarounds are velocity-dependent restitution plus learned residual contact models, system ID of contact-initiation parameters, keeping force *direction* (robust) separate from force *magnitude* (fragile), and real-world fine-tuning/tactile BC on top of sim RL.

### Cited Findings
- van Steen et al. 2024: AGX Dynamics (nonsmooth mechanics) was chosen because it needs no contact stiffness/damping estimation; rigid impact maps matched hardware post-impact velocities to 3.1% average error, but "impact-induced vibrations" contaminate velocity signals so evaluation must be done within the RS control framework — [arXiv 2411.06319 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06319v1)
- Aouaj et al. 2021: rigid-body impact maps predict no oscillation while the real 7-DOF arm shows "damped oscillatory response after the impact" — [arXiv 2010.08220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08220)
- Tang et al. ICRA 2024: "MuJoCo cannot simulate the complex interactions between the screw and the substrate," so the screw was modeled as a single rotational DOF with rising resistance torque — [ICRA 2024 PDF](https://www.cs.iastate.edu/files/inline-files/icra24.pdf)
- Atar, Huang, Richter, Yip, "In-Hand Manipulation of Articulated Tools with Dexterous Robot Hands with Sim-to-Real Transfer" (2025/26): articulated tools (scissors, pliers, surgical instruments, staplers) expose "under-modeled joint phenomena such as friction, stiction, and backlash" and imperfect tactile sensing; fix = sim-trained base policy plus a "sensor-driven refinement learned from hardware demonstrations" fusing proprioception, target articulation and whole-hand tactile/F-T via cross-attention — [arXiv 2509.23075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23075)
- Hsieh et al. ICRA 2026: cites "the difficulty of simulating complex contact dynamics and multisensory signals, especially tactile feedback" as the reason to add real tactile BC after sim RL for screwdriving — [arXiv 2512.02011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02011)
- PLUME (2026): "standard domain randomization strategies may be insufficient for precise tasks"; instead infer object shape/pose/friction online — [arXiv 2606.11396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11396)
- Conti et al. (Sony AI), "Physics Models for Sim-to-Real Transfer in Professional-Level Robot Table Tennis" (2026): ball state from nine cameras at 200 Hz; racket impact modeled with velocity-dependent normal restitution e_r = 0.878 − 0.020·|v_n|, tangential e_t = 0.819 − 0.010·|v_T|, spin damping 0.805, plus a residual neural network in the racket frame (8-D input: relative velocity, spin, contact location); median landing error cut from 0.37 m to 0.15 m (−59%); "the major cause of the sim-to-real gap … is the racket contact model," and accuracy "deteriorates significantly" for off-center impacts; racket state estimation errors ≈ 8 mm, 0.2 m/s, 0.7° — [arXiv 2606.28805 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28805)
- Google's robotic table-tennis system grid-searched the paddle restitution coefficient (used to approximate softness) for best zero-shot transfer — [arXiv 2309.03315](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.03315); badminton striking work randomizes kinematic/dynamic parameters of arm and shuttlecock for zero-shot transfer — [Frontiers in Neurorobotics 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurorobotics/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2025.1649870/full)
- Yang et al., "Direction Matters: Learning Force Direction Enables Sim-to-Real Contact-Rich Manipulation" (2026): "force magnitudes … are highly sensitive to simulation inaccuracies, [whereas] force directions encode high-level task geometry and remain robust across the sim-to-real gap"; magnitudes tuned with "a single scalar per contact state"; four real tasks — [arXiv 2602.14174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14174)
- Dierking et al. 2026: on hardware, "contact-initiation parameters yield interpretable adaptation signals, whereas global physics parameters provide feedback that is too weak" — [arXiv 2606.20712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20712)
- Grasp-to-Act 2026 achieves zero-shot sim-to-real for real hammer strikes with a proprioception-only RL grasp adapter (no tactile to transfer) — [arXiv 2602.20466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20466)
- General sim-to-real DR survey for context — [arXiv 2009.13303](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.13303)

### Inferences
- For hammering, the velocity-jump part of the impact is simulable (van Steen), but the parts a tactile grip regulator would act on — post-impact ringing, hammer–hand micro-slip, nail–wood penetration resistance — are exactly the parts no cited simulator captures; a residual/learned contact model (as in Sony's racket model) or real-world fine-tuning is the demonstrated remedy.
- The "direction robust / magnitude fragile" finding suggests a policy should learn *when/where* to regulate grip and torque in sim, while calibrating *how much* on hardware.

### Gaps
- No primary source was retrieved that directly benchmarks MuJoCo vs Isaac Sim vs Drake vs Genesis on impact restitution or vibration fidelity; claims here are per-paper workarounds rather than a controlled comparison.
- No paper reports simulating sustained tool vibration (drill chatter) for policy learning.

## Key Question 7: Tool as an intermediate body — extended body schema, tool-dynamics identification, learned tool affordance/dynamics (KETO, ToolFlowNet, RoboTool, tool-body assimilation)

### Takeaway
Tool-as-extension work is split between body-schema/dynamics-identification methods (Nabeshima 2006; motor-babbling inertia estimation; Ogata-lab tool-body assimilation; vibration sensing through tools) and affordance-keypoint methods (KETO, GIFT, ToolFlowNet, Ti/Calinon manipulability affordance), with LLM tool use (RoboTool) sitting on top; none of these models the tool's *dynamic* coupling to a target under impact, and the affordance methods that hammer do so with open-loop or vision-only execution.

### Cited Findings
- Nabeshima, Kuniyoshi, Lungarella, "Adaptive body schema for robotic tool-use," Advanced Robotics 2006 — [PDF](https://bme.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/917/2021/08/Nabeshima_Advanced-Robotics_2006.pdf)
- A 2022 Frontiers survey of body-schema extension: the schema "can plastically be modulated to include other objects such as a tool" only when the agent actively uses it ("this plasticity property does not exist when the tool is passively held"); robots have predicted "tool inertia parameters through tool-held motor babbling, computing from observed forces and torques when swinging tools" — [Frontiers in Robotics and AI 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2022.1009488/xml)

- Hiruma, Ito, Mori, Ogata, "Deep Active Visual Attention for Real-time Robot Motion Generation: Emergence of Tool-body Assimilation and Adaptive Tool-use" (2022): role-based attention "perceived the robot grippers and tool as identical end-effectors" during picking/dragging; stable with untrained tools and distractions — [arXiv 2206.14530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14530)
- Taunyazov et al. 2021 — vibration sensing *through* grasped tools with dynamic tactile sensors (impact localization on a rod) — [arXiv 2106.00489](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00489)

- Turpin, Wang, Tsogkas, Dickinson, Garg, "GIFT: Generalizable Interaction-aware Functional Tool Affordances without Labels" (2021): sparse keypoint affordances for hooking, reaching and hammering; "matches a human oracle on two of three tasks using novel tools" — [arXiv 2106.14973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14973)

- Ti et al. 2024 formalize hammer affordance as a directional velocity-manipulability cost inside optimal control (KQ2) — [arXiv 2402.05502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05502)
- Phys2Real (2025) fuses VLM priors with online estimation of physical parameters (e.g., a hammer's off-center mass) for planar pushing — [arXiv 2510.11689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11689)
- Tool-as-Interface (CoRL 2025) makes the tool pose itself the policy interface (tool-centric SE(3) actions tracked by FoundationPose), enabling human-to-robot transfer for hammering — [arXiv 2504.04612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04612)

### Inferences
- Existing tool-as-extension work either identifies tool inertia offline (babbling/swinging) or treats the tool as a kinematic extension for perception; none identifies tool–target coupling online (e.g., whether the drill bit is engaged, whether the hammer face is square to the nail) from tactile/effort signals — the "second body" in the user's framing is unmodeled.

### Gaps
- KETO's real-robot hammering execution details (robot, whether motions were scripted) were not extractable from the abstract.
- No work found on learned dynamics of the *tool–target* pair (nail-in-wood penetration, screw thread engagement) as a state estimate for control.

## Key Question 8 (explicit flag): Does ANY work combine a learned high-level policy with a high-frequency controller for hammering or drilling?

### Takeaway
No. Across all sources retrieved, no system combines a learned high-level policy (VLA/world model/diffusion) with a high-frequency (≥100 Hz) force- or tactile-driven low-level controller for hammering or drilling; the nearest neighbors are Grasp-to-Act (30 Hz proprioceptive RL grip adapter under an open-loop hammer swing), Opt2VLA/FAVLA/Helix (fast-slow architectures on non-tool tasks), and Torobo (kHz-class torque/impedance control absorbing hammer rebound but no learned layer).

### Cited Findings
- Grasp-to-Act (RA-L 2026): learned (RL) grasp adaptation at 30 Hz from proprioception during real 4-strike nail driving, but the arm trajectory is an open-loop human demonstration and there is no tactile sensing or high-level policy — [arXiv 2602.20466 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20466)
- Tool-as-Interface (CoRL 2025) and PoCo (RSS 2024): learned diffusion policies hammer on real robots at 6–10 Hz with vision (PoCo adds one GelSight finger) and no fast force loop — [arXiv 2504.04612](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04612); [arXiv 2402.02511](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02511v3)
- Torobo (2024): joint-torque/impedance control at all arm joints absorbs hammer rebound; no learned policy reported — [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/innovation/torobo-humanoid-robot-hammers-nail)
- Opt2VLA (2026): 5 Hz VLA → 200 Hz force-aware RL WBC → 2 kHz PD on Digit, but tasks are wiping/pushing/pickup — [arXiv 2609.23968](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23968)
- Li et al., "FAVLA: A Force-Adaptive Fast–Slow VLA model" (Feb 2026): Monte dual-arm (7-DoF X-ARM) with 6-axis F/T at the flange sampled at 200 Hz (downsampled to 30 Hz for training); slow VLM synchronized with vision at ~30 Hz, fast action expert scheduled 1×–N_max× based on predicted force variance; explicitly motivated by "delayed responses to impacts, stick-slip, and force spikes" in unified-frequency VLAs; tasks = USB insertion, gear assembly (93.3%), box flipping, board wiping; overall 80.8% (+13.8% over ForceVLA); peak contact forces 7.7–9.9 N vs 12–13 N baselines; failure modes include force saturation safety stops and slipping — [arXiv 2602.23648 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23648v1)
- ForceVLA (NeurIPS 2025): force-aware MoE fusing 6-axis F/T during action decoding; +23.2% over baselines, up to 80% on plug insertion; no tool tasks — [arXiv 2505.22159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22159)
- Tactile-VLA (2025): VLA with a "hybrid position-force controller" and tactile reasoning; generic contact-rich tasks, no hammering/drilling — [arXiv 2507.09160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09160); TaF-VLA aligns tactile observations with interaction forces — [arXiv 2601.20321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20321); FD-VLA distills force — [arXiv 2602.02142](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.02142)
- LIFT, "Never Too Late for Force" (CoRL 2026): reactive action expert with causal force memory over 6-D end-effector force; towel folding, book insertion, Hanoi ring — [arXiv 2607.14236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14236)
- Helmut, Funk, Schneider, de Farias, Peters, "Tactile-Conditioned Diffusion Policy for Force-Aware Robotic Manipulation" (2025): UMI-style gripper with GelSight Mini; policy jointly predicts pose, grip width and grip force; three force tasks, none percussive — [arXiv 2510.13324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13324)
- DuoCore-FS asynchronous fast-slow VLA: fast pathway at 30 Hz whole-body action chunks, vision only — [arXiv 2512.20188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20188)
- Figure Helix: System 2 at 7–9 Hz, System 1 at 200 Hz outputting wrist poses, finger flexion/abduction, torso/head targets; "no mention of force or tactile sensing" — [Figure AI](https://www.figure.ai/news/helix)
- Survey: Shan et al., "Learning Physical Interaction: A Survey of Tactile- and Force-aware Robot Learning" (2026, 53 pp.) notes many systems "adopt multi-phase architectures that combine high-level policies, action-refinement modules, and low-level controllers" — [arXiv 2608.07558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07558)
- Audio as a contact-event channel: ManiWAV (CoRL 2024) uses an "ear-in-hand" contact microphone for passive sensing of contact events/modes and active sensing of materials — [arXiv 2406.19464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19464)

### Inferences
- The fast-slow force-aware VLA literature (FAVLA, Opt2VLA, LIFT) already argues the user's thesis — high-frequency force feedback must bypass the slow VLM — but has only been validated on insertion/wiping/pushing with peak forces ≈10 N, roughly an order of magnitude below hammer-strike loads and without percussive timing constraints.
- Combining Grasp-to-Act-style grip adaptation (upgraded with tactile), RS-style ante/post-impact reference handling at 1 kHz, and a 5–10 Hz learned strike-planning policy would be a new combination; each ingredient exists on hardware separately.

### Gaps
- No public work reports a tactile-in-the-loop hammer or drill controller at ≥100 Hz; none reports grip-force modulation timed to a strike or to drill engagement.
- Whether any 2026 humanoid vendor demo (beyond Torobo) uses learned control for hammering could not be verified from primary sources.
