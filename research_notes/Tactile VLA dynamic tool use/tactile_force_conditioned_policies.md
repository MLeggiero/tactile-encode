# Tactile- and Force-Conditioned Learned Robot Policies (VLAs, force-aware IL, representation learning, data collection, grip-force regulation, rates)

Scope note: research conducted 2026-09-28 from primary sources (arXiv abstracts/HTML, project pages, GitHub READMEs, one 53-method survey PDF). Classification used throughout: **(i)** tactile/force as observation only; **(ii)** tactile/force used to modulate compliance/impedance or to trigger reactive corrections; **(iii)** explicit force / wrench / grip-force action outputs. Rates are quoted in Hz whenever the source states them; "not stated" otherwise.

## Key Question 1: Tactile/force VLAs and foundation-model policies (2024–2026) — what exactly do they do with force, and do any output impedance/stiffness or grip-force commands?

### Takeaway
Nearly all tactile/force VLAs (TLA, VTLA, OmniVTLA, ForceVLA, TacVLA, TaF-VLA, TA-VLA, TAP-VLA, T-Rex, VTAM, Dream-Tac, VT-WAM, FM-VLA, TacForcing, MoSS, AT-VLA, VLA-Touch) inject tactile/force as extra tokens or gated conditioning into a π0/π0.5/RDT/GO-1/Qwen backbone and still emit **position-only** action chunks (class i), typically at ~8–11 Hz where stated. The exceptions that emit force-domain commands are Tactile-VLA (target contact force + gripper-width channel regulating internal grasp force, executed by a hybrid position–force controller), Tabero-VTLA (decoupled force–position commands to a fixed hybrid controller), and "Compliance for Free" (VLA fine-tuned to output per-axis stiffness with pose); none of them addresses impacts or vibration, and none targets hammering or drilling.

### Cited Findings
- **Tactile-VLA** (Huang, Wang, Lin, Hu, Wen, Gao; Tsinghua/UESTC/SJTU; arXiv July 2025): dual high-resolution tactile sensors capturing normal and shear force on a modified UMI gripper; tactile history of H measurements fused by "a simple MLP" into a single token with token-level fusion in the transformer; the model "explicitly outputs both target position and target contact force"; a hybrid position–force controller converts force targets into position adjustments when force error exceeds a threshold; "the gripper's Cartesian position is used to exclusively regulate the net external force applied to an object, while the gripper width is used in parallel to control the internal grasping force"; sensing at "100Hz tactile feedback and 20Hz visual data"; flow-matching loss on "both the kinematic and force dimensions"; results: USB insertion 35% vs 0–5% (π0), charger 90% vs 25–40%, fragile OOD grasp 90% vs 0–10%, blackboard wiping with CoT 80% vs 0%; "softly" vs "hard" produced 0.51 N vs 2.57 N; policy inference Hz not stated — [arXiv 2507.09160](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09160). Class **(iii)**.
- **TLA: Tactile-Language-Action** (Hao, Zhang et al.; Samsung Research China / CASIA / BAAI; arXiv Mar 2025): simulated GelStereo 2.0 in Isaac Gym with FEM; 8 tactile images (L/R fingertip × 4 time steps) tiled into a 3×3 composite at 616×616, encoded by Qwen2-VL's ViT, compressed by an MLP, concatenated with language tokens into Qwen2-7B; outputs Δx, Δy, Δrz as tokens via next-token prediction; 24k tactile–action–instruction pairs; insertion success 96%/94%/74% at 2.0/1.6/1.0 mm clearance vs best DP 58/43/20%; OOD peg shapes 90–96%; control/inference Hz not stated; authors list "the model does not rigorously capture tactile temporal information" and no sim-to-real evaluation as limitations — [arXiv 2503.08548](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08548v1). Class **(i)**. A bimanual follow-up, BiTLA, appears in an ACM MM 2025 workshop — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3728485.3759237).
- **VTLA** (arXiv May 2025; also Elsevier 2026): simulated vision–tactile–action–instruction dataset for fingertip insertion; Direct Preference Optimization added "to offer regression-like supervision"; >90% success on unseen peg shapes, beating diffusion policy and TLA/VLA baselines — [arXiv 2505.09577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09577); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667379726000616). Class **(i)**; rates not stated.
- **VLA-Touch** (Bi, Ma, Hao, Shou, Soh; NUS; arXiv Jul 2025): base VLA (RDT-1B) is not fine-tuned; GelSight Mini (7×9 markers) → marker-tracking force vector; high level uses the Octopi tactile-language model to turn 6-frame tactile clips into text for GPT-4o planning; low level is a BRIDGeR interpolant (diffusion-style) controller that refines VLA action chunks with tactile + DinoV2 features; controller runs at **8 Hz** on an RTX 4090 and "does not yet fully leverage high-frequency tactile signals (≥25 Hz)"; tasks: cup (water detection), sponge wiping, mango peeling with a hand-held peeler; e.g., wipe 12/20 vs 5/20, peel 10/20 vs 6/20 — [arXiv 2507.17294](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.17294v1). Class **(i)** (action refinement, no force outputs).
- **OmniVTLA** (Cheng et al.; SJTU + Paxini Tech; arXiv v3 June 2026): force-based Paxini Gen2 Hall-effect tactile arrays; force maps normalized into 3-channel 224×224 images; dual-path encoder = SigLIP ViT + semantically-aligned SA-ViT trained contrastively on ObjTac (135K tri-modal samples, 56 objects, 60 Hz force, 30 FPS video); π0 backbone (Gemma-2.6B, flow-matching head); actions = 3 relative positions + 6 relative angles + 1 gripper state (or 16 finger joints for a 4-finger hand) — no gripper-force command; pick-and-place 96.9% vs 75.0% (gripper), 100% vs 93.8% (hand); peg insertion 83.3% vs 50.0%; inference Hz not stated — [arXiv 2508.08706](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08706). Class **(i)**.
- **ForceVLA** (Yu, Liu, Yu, Ren et al.; SJTU et al.; NeurIPS 2025): 6-axis F/T at the end-effector "synchronized with vision streams at 30 FPS"; force linearly projected into tokens and fused after the VLM by FVLMoE, a sparse MoE (4 experts, top-1); π0 (PaliGemma) base; outputs TCP pose + gripper width — "positional outputs only; no explicit force/impedance commands"; 60.5% vs 37.3% average success (+23.2%); tasks: bottle pumping, plug insertion, USB insertion, whiteboard wiping, cucumber peeling; ForceVLA-Data = 244 trajectories / 140k timesteps on a Flexiv Rizon via Quest 3 teleop; limitation: relies on "estimated external wrench values"; control Hz not stated — [arXiv 2505.22159](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22159v1); [NeurIPS page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/120169). Class **(i)**.
- **TacVLA** (Zhang et al.; Purdue + IIT; arXiv Mar 2026): 15×8 tactile array (120 taxels) at **10 Hz**; 36 tactile tokens via MLP + 2D positional embeddings; binary contact gate zeroes tactile tokens when taxel count under threshold; π0.5 fine-tuned with LoRA; flow-matching action chunks; no grip-force outputs; disassembly 83.75% vs 63.75% (π0.5), in-box picking 70% vs 10%, front-camera-blocked 60% vs ~30%; gating ablation 83.75→71.25%; inference Hz not stated — [arXiv 2603.12665](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12665v1). Class **(i)**.
- **TaF-VLA** (Huang, Lin, Li et al.; arXiv Jan 2026): 4 custom marker-based VBTS + 2 GelSight Mini at 30 Hz, ATI Axia80 6-axis F/T and a 12×12 piezoresistive matrix; automated indentation rig yields "100,000 synchronized tactile-force frames per hour", >10M paired frames over 6 sensors; TaF-Adapter = VQ-VAE codebooks for pressure maps and F/T (window N=5) + ViT + causal transformer, aligned by InfoNCE; frozen adapter injects force-aligned tactile tokens into π0.5; outputs 7-D end-effector velocity trajectories, "no explicit force outputs"; 8 tasks (tube insertion, tweezer weight pick, power-bank extraction, whiteboard erasing, jelly slicing, heavy part lifting, tongs food pick, chocolate grasping); 64.8% vs 37.1% (π0.5) vs 42.8% (tactile-vision-aligned baseline); limitation: receding-horizon output limits "high-frequency dynamic events"; Hz not stated — [arXiv 2601.20321](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20321). Class **(i)**.
- **TA-VLA** (Zhang et al.; BAAI / AIR-Tsinghua / NTU; CoRL 2025): joint torques derived from motor currents on a Cobot Magic ALOHA (7-DoF arms; also ROKAE SR); torque history over 2 s (~10 frames) summarized into a single token by an MLP and inserted into the **decoder** (not encoder); auxiliary torque-prediction loss L_action + β·L_torque; π0 (and RDT-1B); 50-step chunks; inference ≈ **11 Hz** (90–94 ms/step on RTX 4090); contact-rich tasks (button pushing, charger/USB plugging, socket unplugging, door-handle turning) 15–18/20 vs 0–5/20 for π0; limitation: current-based torque "can be affected by motor calibration, sensor noise, or thermal drift" — [arXiv 2509.07962](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07962); [GitHub](https://github.com/ZZongzheng0918/TA-VLA). Class **(i)**.
- **TAP-VLA** (Van der Merwe et al.; U. Michigan; arXiv June 2026): GelSight on both fingers; shear fields from optical flow are rendered as vector overlays on the RGB images the π0.5 policy already consumes (no new input channel); 8-D action (7 delta joints + binary gripper), 10-step horizon, demos at ~10 Hz; 78% (94/120) vs <50% for all baselines on mass discrimination (38 g vs 422 g), balancing, gear and plug insertion; limitation: overlays discard grasp geometry and occlude clutter — [arXiv 2606.29089](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29089v1). Class **(i)**.
- **T-Rex** (Niu et al., 34 authors incl. Fei-Fei Li, Goldberg, Malik, Abbeel, Darrell; arXiv June 2026): Dexmate Vega-1 bimanual robot with Sharpa Wave 22-DoF hands, 5 fingertip sensors per hand giving 6-D force vectors and depth maps; **tactile at 300 Hz (low-level control rate), policy at 30 Hz**, decoupled asynchronously; variable-rate Mixture-of-Transformers: low-frequency action expert over vision/language, high-frequency tactile expert triggered at intra-chunk offsets {0,4,8,12} of 16-step chunks using cached visual context; per-finger temporal VQ-VAE over force history f_{t-15:t} (codebook 64, magnitude-weighted MSE); Qwen3VL-2B backbone; 62-D actions (EE deltas + absolute finger joints); 100-hour teleop dataset, 200+ objects, 22 motor primitives; 65% vs 35% (EgoScale) over 12 tasks incl. Screw Lightbulb and Open Lock; limitation: "sensor distortion, calibration drift across devices, and the absence of dense palm sensing" — [arXiv 2606.17055](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17055); [project](https://tactile-rex.github.io/). Class **(i)**.
- **AT-VLA** (Li et al.; arXiv May 2026): Xense tactile sensors on an AgiBot Genie1; resultant 3-D normal + 3-D tangential force; GO-1 base (Intern-VL-2B + DiT); learnable tactile gate (contact vs non-contact, BCE-supervised) and adaptive cross-attention that swaps the query source between state and tactile tokens; dual-stream slow VLM / fast tactile stream at a **3:1 frequency ratio** with "real-time close-loop responses within 0.04 s"; outputs 14-DoF bimanual EE poses; contact-rich success 50% vs 22% (GO-1); failure note: "occasional gripper slippage when grip firmness isn't optimal" on Unscrew Lid — [arXiv 2605.07308](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07308). Class **(i)** (fast stream corrects motion, no force command).
- **VTAM** (Yuan et al.; arXiv Mar 2026): pretrained video transformer + tactile streams via "lightweight modality transfer finetuning" and a tactile regularization loss enforcing balanced cross-modal attention; 90% average success; +80% over π0.5 on potato-chip pick-and-place — [arXiv 2603.23481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23481). Class **(i)**.
- **Dream-Tac** (Lou et al.; arXiv June 2026): unified tactile world–action model with contact-gated visuotactile fusion and contact-aware attention bias; +31.7% action accuracy on 6 tasks; 2.9× faster training, 1.8× faster inference — [arXiv 2606.08737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08737). **VT-WAM** (Tian et al.; arXiv Jul 2026): predicts visual + tactile deformation + action with asymmetric MoT attention and contact-gated AVTAG; 71.67% average over 6 real tasks, +35.84 pts over OmniVTLA — [arXiv 2607.02503](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02503). **DreamTacVLA** (Ye et al.; arXiv Dec 2025/Jun 2026): tactile world model predicting future tactile signals; "up to 95% success" — [arXiv 2512.23864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23864). All class **(i)**; rates not stated.
- **FM-VLA** (Li et al.; arXiv Jul 2026): force histories encoded to memory tokens by a VAE pretrained on force time series; solves visually ambiguous repetition tasks (hidden block, pressing buttons N times, wiping N times) with >80% success — [arXiv 2607.18231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18231). Class **(i)**.
- **TacForcing** (Zhou et al.; arXiv Aug 2026): streaming action expert conditioned on evolving tactile observations; Execution-Aware Tactile Attention restricts tactile conditioning to actions nearing execution; 65% (6 sim UniVTAC tasks) / 69% (3 real) — [arXiv 2608.25798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25798). Class **(i)**; rates not stated.
- **MoSS** (Lee et al.; CoRL 2026): decoupled modality streams for tactile, force and torque fused into the action stream via joint cross-modal self-attention; two-stage training preserving pretrained VLA weights, then auxiliary prediction of future physical signals — [arXiv 2604.23272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23272). Class **(i)**.
- **TacCoRL** (Ma et al.; arXiv June 2026): "real-aligned simulator" + RL teaches a VLA how tactile should modify actions in near-failure states; 72.5% vs 50.0% on 4 bimanual tasks — [arXiv 2606.11743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11743). Class **(i)**.
- **FD-VLA** (Zhao et al.; ICRA 2026): no F/T sensor at inference; a learnable query token predicts a force token aligned with the latent of real force signals; authors claim the distilled token "outperforms direct sensor force measurements" — [arXiv 2602.02142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02142). **HapticVLA** (Gubernatorov et al.; arXiv Mar 2026): tactile used only at training as rewards "penalizing excessive grasping force" (SA-RWFM) plus tactile distillation; 86.7% mean real-world success and reportedly beats VLAs given tactile at inference — [arXiv 2603.15257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15257). **CRAFT** (Zhang et al.; arXiv Feb 2026): force-first curriculum via a variational information bottleneck on vision/language embeddings, with data from a homologous leader–follower teleop rig with synchronized force — [arXiv 2602.12532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12532). All class (i)-adjacent (force at training time).
- **FWBC-VLA** (Zhang et al.; arXiv Sep 2026): sensorless residual-torque estimator (HSR-Force) encodes contact onset/loading/release as tokens injected into the VLA action expert; a whole-body compensation generator on a wheeled-legged robot adds corrective actions; tasks: whiteboard wiping, door opening against a closer; WL&Arm dataset >5,000 episodes; loop rate not stated — [arXiv 2609.03889](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03889). Class **(ii)**.
- **VisForce** (Lee & Lim; arXiv Sep 2026): RH56F1 dexterous hand with fingertip force sensing; current and desired forces rendered onto wrist/goal images with goal-conditioned cross-attention; "force-aware actions rather than explicit force commands"; grasp-and-lift 70% (egg)/80% (toothpaste); slip-modulated peg-in-hole 40% — [arXiv 2609.25785](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25785). Class **(i)**.
- **Tabero-VTLA** (Wu et al.; arXiv May 2026): "decoupled force-position command interface; the resulting force-position commands are executed by a fixed hybrid controller"; reduces average grip force by >70% under "gentle" instructions while keeping success; Hz not stated — [arXiv 2605.27886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27886). Class **(iii)**.
- **Compliance for Free** (Guda, Colomé, Torras; arXiv Sep 2026): 4-channel bilateral teleoperation with joint-torque sensing makes per-axis stiffness identifiable by regression; a VLA is fine-tuned "to output stiffness alongside pose"; Franka Research 3 wiping; the only policy of five whose contact force changes with a "firm" instruction (6.4 N → 9.1 N RMS, Cohen's d = 0.89, p = 0.023) — [arXiv 2609.19976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19976). Class **(ii)/(iii)** (stiffness output).
- **ForceFlow** (Zhang et al.; arXiv May 2026): flow-matching policy with asymmetric fusion treating force "as a global regulatory signal" and a Vision-to-Force handover; +37% success over ForceVLA on 6 real tasks; Hz not stated — [arXiv 2605.11048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11048).
- **GelFusion** (Jiang et al.; arXiv May 2025): GelSight; dual-channel "texture-geometric and dynamic interaction features" with vision-dominated cross-attention; surface wiping, peg insertion, fragile pick-and-place — [arXiv 2505.07455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07455). **Touch in the Wild** (Zhu, Huang, Li; arXiv Jul 2025): portable tactile gripper + cross-modal encoder; test-tube insertion and pipetting — [arXiv 2507.15062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15062). Both class (i) diffusion-style policies, not VLAs.
- The 53-method survey "Learning Physical Interaction" (Shan et al., 2026) reports that among its corpus "18 use wrist F/T sensing and 9 use joint torque sensing, with 2 using both), 27 use tactile sensors, 4 combine a force-domain sensor with a tactile sensor" and that "recent tactile- and force-aware policies sometimes explicitly predict reference force signals … ranging from Cartesian wrenches to grasping forces" — [arXiv 2608.07558](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07558).

### Inferences
- The dominant design pattern is "force/tactile → compact token(s) → concatenate/gate into a pretrained VLA → position action chunk at ~10 Hz". Force enters as an *observation*; the physics of force regulation is left to the position controller. Only Tactile-VLA, Tabero and Compliance-for-Free move a force-domain quantity into the *action space* of a VLA.
- Several 2026 works (TacVLA, AT-VLA, Dream-Tac, VT-WAM) converge on **contact gating** — tactile only affects attention when contact is detected — which is a plausible template for a "high-energy contact phase" switch in a hammering/drilling controller.
- T-Rex is the only VLA found that ingests tactile at ≥200 Hz (300 Hz), and it does so by *compressing* a 16-sample history into VQ tokens for a 30 Hz policy — i.e., the high rate is used for perception, not for control bandwidth.
- No tactile/force VLA reports handling impacts or vibration; TaF-VLA explicitly names receding-horizon latency as a limit for "high-frequency dynamic events".

### Gaps
- Inference/control frequency is not stated for TLA, VTLA, OmniVTLA, ForceVLA, TaF-VLA, TacVLA, Tactile-VLA, Dream-Tac, VT-WAM, TacForcing, MoSS, Tabero, ForceFlow, VisForce (only VLA-Touch 8 Hz, TA-VLA ~11 Hz, T-Rex 30 Hz, AT-VLA 0.04 s loop are explicit).
- Whether TLA was deployed on real hardware is ambiguous in the fetched text (per-clearance "robotic insertion" tables exist, but the limitations state real-world evaluation is future work); the HTML should be re-read before citing real-robot numbers.
- Affiliations/venues for VTLA, GelFusion, Tabero, ForceFlow and several 2026 preprints were not exposed in the fetched abstracts.
- No VLA with a dedicated *gripper-force action head* (a scalar force output for a parallel gripper) was found besides Tactile-VLA's gripper-width channel and Tabero's force command; FARM/UMI-FT/Feel-the-Force have such heads but are diffusion/transformer IL policies, not VLAs.

## Key Question 2: Force- and tactile-conditioned imitation learning / diffusion policies (including slow-fast and reactive variants) — which output stiffness/impedance/force targets, and at what rates?

### Takeaway
The force-aware IL literature splits into (a) reactive slow-fast architectures that keep position actions but close a fast tactile/force loop at 10–30 Hz (RDP 1–2 Hz latent / 24 Hz fast; ImplicitRDP 10 Hz; FA-RDP 10→30 Hz with a 100 Hz force-compensation layer; FoAR 10 Hz policy / 100 Hz position control with a rule-based force check), and (b) policies that output force-domain targets executed by compliance controllers: TacDiffusion (6-D wrench → 1 kHz impedance loop with feed-forward), ACP/UMI-FT (pose + virtual target + stiffness [+ grasp force] → 500 Hz admittance), ForceMimic (wrench + hybrid force–position primitive), FARM (target grip force → 25 Hz PID), Feel-the-Force (desired contact force → PD gripper), and Bi-ACT (joint torques at ~100 Hz on a 1 kHz bilateral loop). None models impacts; FoAR's chopping is the only explicit impact-like task.

### Cited Findings
- **Reactive Diffusion Policy (RDP)** (Xue, Ren, Chen, Zhang, Fang, Gu, Xu, Lu; SJTU/Tsinghua/Qi Zhi/Shanghai AI Lab; RSS 2025): sensors GelSight Mini (25 FPS, 7×9 markers), MCTac (30 FPS, 5×7 markers), and Flexiv Rizon 4 "built-in joint torque sensors (120Hz streamed, downsampled to 24 FPS)"; **slow latent diffusion policy at 1–2 Hz; fast asymmetric-tokenizer policy at 24 FPS** closed-loop on tactile; tactile = marker optical flow → 3-D deformation field → 15-D PCA; 6-D wrench concatenated to observations; actions = relative EE trajectories (no force/stiffness output); TactAR teleop on Quest 3 (~$500 + $549 GelSight Mini / ~$50 MCTac); tasks peeling, wiping, bimanual lifting; >35% improvement; peeling with perturbation 0.80 vs 0.15 (DP); limitations: "fast policy cannot process high-frequency visual inputs", two-finger grippers only, no vibration/impact handling — [arXiv 2503.02881](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02881); [GitHub](https://github.com/xiaoxiaoxh/reactive_diffusion_policy). The survey characterizes RDP's fast module as "an asymmetric tokenizer based on CNN/GRU modules to decode latent chunks at 20–30 Hz" — [survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07558). Class **(ii)** (reactive position refinement).
- **ImplicitRDP** (Chen, Xue et al.; RA-L 2026): single end-to-end visual–force policy; 6-axis F/T at TCP sampled at **10 Hz**, vision at 1–2 Hz, action/control at **10 Hz**; GRU force encoding + causal attention masks so an action can attend only to current/past force tokens; relative Cartesian actions; tasks box flipping (<14 N constraint), switch toggling, 1 mm-margin hole inserting; 18/20, 18/20, 8/20 vs RDP 16/10/5 and DP 0/8/0; ~30 ms inference; 40 demos/task — [arXiv 2512.10946](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10946). Class **(ii)**.
- **FA-RDP** (Zhuo, Chen, Xue, Tang, Lv, Lu, Wen; arXiv Jul 2026): end-effector F/T at 30 Hz; **low-frequency mode 10 Hz with 16-step chunks; high-frequency one-step mode 30 Hz with 48-step chunks** (both a 1.6 s horizon); a learned multimodality indicator switches modes once contact reduces action ambiguity; "a 100 Hz compensation layer that linearly interpolates policy outputs and adjusts each command using the latest measured world-frame external force"; 81.7% vs ImplicitRDP 51.7% vs DP 10.0% on dual box flipping / switch toggling / button pressing — [arXiv 2607.28596](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28596). Class **(ii)**.
- **FoAR** (He, Fang, Chen, Fang, Lu; SJTU; RA-L 2025): OptoForce F/T between flange and gripper at **100 Hz**, 200-step (~2 s) history; MLP → 512-D force tokens + transformer; a future-contact predictor φ(t) (ResNet18 + MLP, BCE on auto-labelled contact) weights force features h = [h_s; φ·h_f + (1−φ)·h*]; actions 7-D (pos + quat + width), **policy 10 Hz, executed by a 100 Hz end-effector position controller**; reactive rule: if φ>0.9 and |F|<8 N or |τ|<5 N·m, step ε = 0.006 m toward the predicted direction over 5 steps; tasks wiping (100% vs 75%), cucumber peeling (100% vs 50%), pepper **chopping** — described as "instantaneous force impact" — 70% / 3.9 segments vs 30% / 1.8; hardware Flexiv Rizon + Dahuan AG-95; future work: "compliance control and hybrid force/position control" — [arXiv 2411.15753](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15753v1); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=cbjluXVaJz). Class **(ii)** (position-only actions with force-triggered correction).
- **ForceMimic / HybridIL** (Liu, Wang, Wang, Wang, Lu; SJTU; arXiv Oct 2024, IEEE 2025): ForceCapture handheld device: 6-axis F/T at **1000 Hz**, RealSense T265 SLAM at 200 Hz, L515 RGB-D 30 Hz, jaw encoder 30 Hz, ~$50 printed parts, 0.8 kg incl. 0.5 kg sensor, gravity-compensated; HybridIL (modified diffusion policy on point clouds + TCP history) predicts EE pose **and 6-axis wrench** 20 steps ahead; primitive switch at 6 N: ≤6 N → IK joint-position control, >6 N → hybrid force–position control with orthogonality constraint; ~10 Hz action rate implied (30 Hz observations, actions advance 3 steps); zucchini peeling: 100% motion / 85% >10 cm peel vs raw DP 80% / 55%; executed force ~7.5 N vs ~20 N (peaks >40 N) for raw DP; **force-as-input variants performed worse** (domain gap between collection and deployment forces) — [arXiv 2410.07554](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07554v1); [project](https://forcemimic.github.io/). Class **(iii)**.
- **Adaptive Compliance Policy (ACP)** (Hou, Liu, Chi, Cousineau, Kuppuswamy, Feng, Burchfiel, Song; Stanford/TRI; ICRA 2025): ATI mini-45 F/T "7000Hz streaming"; UR5e with GoPro at 60 Hz; **500 Hz Cartesian admittance control**; policy outputs 19-D per arm = 9-D reference pose + 9-D virtual target pose + scalar stiffness in the compliant direction; stiffness labels from 1 s moving-average force with low stiffness along the force direction and piecewise-linear magnitude; diffusion policy with force encoders that are either causal convolutions or **FFT spectrograms through ResNet-18** over 32–256 wrench samples; item flipping 96% vs 23% (compliant) / 14% (stiff); vase wiping 93.75% vs 43.75%; policy Hz not stated; assumes 3-D translation compliance — [arXiv 2410.09309](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09309); [project](https://adaptive-compliance.github.io/). Class **(ii)/(iii)**.
- **UMI-FT** (Choi, Hou, Pan, Hong, Patel, Xu, Cutkosky, Song; arXiv Jan 2026): CoinFT per-finger 6-axis F/T (20 mm × 3 mm) at **360 Hz**; iPhone 15 Pro RGB 60 Hz / depth 30 Hz, ArUco pose at 60 Hz; adaptive compliance policy outputs 21-D = 9-D pose + 9-D virtual target + stiffness + gripper width + **grasp force**; **arm admittance controller at 500 Hz; gripper force controller at 30 Hz** (velocity-resolved admittance); whiteboard wiping 92%, zucchini skewering 80% lab / 100% in-the-wild (20/20 with diverse-scene data vs 4/20 lab-only), lightbulb insertion 95%; limitations: tethered USB, CoinFT delamination under tension — [arXiv 2601.09988](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09988). Class **(iii)**.
- **TacDiffusion** (Wu et al.; TUM MIRMI incl. Haddadin, Knoll; arXiv Sep 2024, IEEE 2025): diffusion policy "generating 6D wrench" executed by an impedance controller with feed-forward force; **policy inference 50–500 Hz depending on model size (DF4 drops to 51.2 Hz); real-time control loop 1000 Hz**; a dynamic-system filter bridges the mismatch, adding 9.15% success; 95.7% zero-shot success on novel sub-millimeter insertions from 1,500 demos — [arXiv 2409.11047](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11047v1); [GitHub](https://github.com/popnut123/TacDiffusion). Class **(iii)**.
- **FARM / Tactile-Conditioned Diffusion Policy** (Helmut, Funk, Schneider, de Farias, Peters; TU Darmstadt/DFKI; arXiv Oct 2025): GelSight Mini rendered as a 3-channel force-distribution image (shear x, y + normal z) into ResNet-18; policy jointly predicts pose, target grip width **and target grip force**; PID force control with anti-windup during contact; **diffusion policy 7 Hz; force loop 25 Hz (GelSight rate); gripper motor ~50 Hz**; tasks plant insertion, grape picking, **screw tightening**; 95–100% vs force-aware 10–95%, tactile-aware 40–65%, vision-only 0–85%; force-distribution W1 0.75 N vs 1.66–5.05 N — [arXiv 2510.13324](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13324v1). Class **(iii)**.
- **FILIC** (Ge et al.; arXiv Sep 2025/Jun 2026): dual-loop transformer IL + impedance torque control; end-effector force estimated from joint torques via Jacobian inversion compensated by a digital-twin model (no F/T sensor); reported to beat vision-only and raw-joint-torque baselines; rates and outputs not stated in abstract — [arXiv 2509.17053](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17053). Class **(ii)**.
- **Bi-ACT** (Buamanee, Kobayashi, Uranishi, Takemura; arXiv Jan 2024): OpenMANIPULATOR-X; **hardware bilateral loop 1000 Hz, policy ~100 Hz, cameras ~200 Hz downsampled to 100 Hz**; torque from disturbance/reaction-force observers on encoders; inputs = 2 images + 15-D (angle, velocity, force × 5 joints); outputs k×15 leader angle/velocity/**torque**; pick-and-place 80–100%, put-in-drawer 100%; force feedback helped on deformable objects — [arXiv 2401.17698](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.17698). Class **(iii)**. A sensorless 4-channel bilateral teleop for low-cost arms shows DOB-estimated force in demonstrations improves IL success — [arXiv 2507.06174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06174).
- **DexForce** (Chen, Yu, Choi, Cutkosky, Bohg; arXiv Jan 2025): contact forces recorded during kinesthetic demos are converted into "force-informed actions"; 76% average over 6 dexterous tasks incl. **unscrewing a nut** vs near-zero for standard actions; force in observations helped most for precision tasks — [arXiv 2501.10356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10356).
- **Feel the Force** (Adeniji, Chen, Liu, Pattabiraman, Bhirangi, Haldar, Abbeel, Pinto; NYU; arXiv June 2025): transformer predicts future EE point-track trajectories **and desired contact forces**; a PD controller "adjusts the gripper closure iteratively until the measured force matches the policy's predicted value"; 77% average over bread, cup unstacking, egg, chips, twist-cap (13/15, 9/15, 13/15, 10/15, 13/15) — [arXiv 2506.01944](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=ti:%22Feel%20the%20Force%22%20AND%20all:tactile&max_results=5); [project](https://feel-the-force-ftf.github.io). Class **(iii)**.
- **Tube Diffusion Policy** (Xue, Rigo, Huang, Shen, Xu, Colonnese, Memar; arXiv Apr 2026): learns "an observation-conditioned feedback flow around nominal action chunks, forming an action tube" for fast visual-tactile corrections with fewer denoising steps; Push-T + three visual-tactile tasks + two real experiments — [arXiv 2604.23609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23609). Class **(ii)**.
- **TactileReflex** (Feng et al.; IROS 2026): rule-based reflex layer, not learned: dual visuo-tactile sensors → shear intensity, contact intensity, center of pressure; three reflex channels (slip suppression, weight-adaptive release, force protection) at **~12 Hz**; dynamic pouring 9/10 vs 0/10 fixed-effort — [arXiv 2605.23568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23568). Class **(ii)**.
- **Multi-Resolution Tactile IL (MiTaS)** (Krohn, Helmut, Funk, Peters, Prasad, Chalvatzaki; arXiv June 2026): "multi-resolution" = **temporal** — GelSight Mini plus the high-frequency event-based Evetac sensor and RGB, fused by modality-specific convolutions + transformer into a flow-matching policy; 80% vs 31% vision-only vs 54% visual-tactile; co-training with Evetac improves >10% on some tasks even when Evetac is absent at test time; rates not stated in abstract — [arXiv 2606.06281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06281).
- **Match lighting** (Funk, Chen, Schneider, Chalvatzaki, Calandra, Peters; arXiv Apr 2025): modular transformer + flow-based generative policy; tactile input improves a dynamic, contact-rich match-lighting task learned from few demos — [arXiv 2504.13618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13618).
- **See, Hear, and Feel** (Li et al.; CoRL 2022): camera + contact microphone + GelSight fused by self-attention; dense packing and pouring; "audio provides immediate feedback of key moments that are even invisible" — [arXiv 2212.03858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03858); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/See,-Hear,-and-Feel:-Smart-Sensory-Fusion-for-Li-Zhang/5666cf5dd2cb1eb55dfa88c1486a6cacdf94c840). **Play it by Ear** (Du, Lee, Nair, Finn; RSS 2022): gripper microphone for contact events under occlusion; 70% success, "50% higher than a policy that does not use audio" — [arXiv 2205.14850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14850). **ManiWAV** (Liu, Chi, Cousineau, Kuppuswamy, Burchfiel, Song; CoRL 2024): piezo contact mic under grip tape streamed via GoPro at **48 kHz**; log-mel spectrograms (16 kHz resample, FFT 400, hop 160, 64 mels) into an Audio Spectrogram Transformer, fused with CLIP ViT-B/16 by a transformer and fed to a diffusion head (16-step chunks); 2–3 s audio history; vision at 20 Hz; flipping 90% vs 25%, wiping 85% vs 40%, pouring 90% vs 0%, taping 80% vs 20%; audio "revealed contact events and modes" — [arXiv 2406.19464](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19464). Class (i).
- **MimicTouch** (Yu, Han, Wang, Saxena, Xu, Zhao; CoRL 2024): human tactile demonstrations + IL + online residual RL to bridge hand→gripper gap for insertion/assembly — [arXiv 2310.16917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16917). **Force-matched IL with a see-through visuotactile sensor** (Ablett et al.; T-RO 2025): reads approximate forces during kinesthetic demos and regenerates trajectories that recreate them; +62.5% success from force matching on door-opening tasks — [arXiv 2311.01248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01248).
- **Tool-as-Interface** (Chen, Zhu, Liu, Li, Driggs-Campbell; arXiv 2025): diffusion policy learned from human tool-use video; **nail hammering 13/13**, pan flipping 12/12; **no force/tactile sensors** — authors write "incorporating tactile sensing could improve performance in contact-intensive tasks"; control Hz not stated — [arXiv 2504.04612](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04612v2).
- Survey observation: diffusion's "iterative denoising process introduces considerable inference latency … making direct deployment in high-frequency closed-loop control challenging", and "regression architecture employs shallow networks … to predict high-frequency reflexive corrections", e.g., "the three-layer MLP in OmniVTA" — [survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07558).

### Inferences
- Where force is *output*, execution rates are decoupled from the policy: TacDiffusion (50–500 Hz policy → 1 kHz impedance), ACP/UMI-FT (policy Hz unstated → 500 Hz admittance + 30 Hz gripper force loop), FARM (7 Hz → 25 Hz PID). This is exactly the "slow policy + fast controller" pattern the user proposes; what is missing everywhere is a fast *learned* loop above ~30 Hz.
- ForceMimic's finding that force-as-*input* baselines underperformed (distribution shift between demo and execution forces) is a caution for any design that feeds raw effort into a slow policy: force is more robustly used as a *target* handed to a controller than as a raw observation.
- FoAR's chopping result (70% with a 10 Hz policy plus a 100 Hz position controller) suggests impact-type tasks are being solved by kinematics with force used only to gate contact — not by regulating the impact itself.

### Gaps
- No force-aware IL work explicitly models vibration or impact transients; "impact" appears in FoAR's chopping only as task description.
- Policy inference rate of ACP and UMI-FT is not stated ("slowest of three parallel loops").
- FILIC's outputs and loop rates were not exposed by the abstract; the full paper would be needed.
- "HACTS" as named in the brief resolved to a *Human-As-Copilot Teleoperation System* (bilateral joint synchronization for intervention data), not a force-centric method — [arXiv 2503.24070](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.24070); no force-domain relation could be verified.
- "Learning force control from demonstrations", "Diffusion policy with impedance" and "Learning to feel" as named in the brief did not resolve to specific papers with the search budget available.

## Key Question 3: Tactile representation learning and cross-modal pretraining — do any encode high-frequency (vibration) content, or only quasi-static images?

### Takeaway
Mainstream tactile encoders (Sparsh, T3, UniTouch, AnyTouch, Octopi, Touch100k, TaF-Adapter, UniForce) are trained on camera-based tactile *images* at 25–60 FPS with at most ~80 ms–5-frame temporal windows; they are quasi-static by construction. The only representation work that explicitly includes vibration-band content is Meta's Sparsh-X on Digit 360 (image + audio + IMU + pressure; Digit 360 senses vibration up to 10 kHz), and outside the "foundation encoder" literature SpectRobot (spectrograms of 0–100 kHz single-point signals) and SlipSense (8 kHz accelerometer fused with a 240 Hz pressure array).

### Cited Findings
- **Sparsh** (Higuera et al.; Meta FAIR; CoRL 2024): SSL (MAE, DINO, JEPA) on 460k+ tactile images for DIGIT (320×240, 60 FPS), GelSight 2017 (25 FPS, markers) and GelSight Mini; inputs are "two concatenated images with stride=5 samples" ≈ **80 ms history**, chosen as "the reaction time that humans need to adjust grip force"; TacBench: T1 force estimation (~36 mN RMSE on DIGIT), T2 slip detection (VJEPA best F1), T3 SE(2) pose, T4 grasp stability (~80%), T5 textile (20 classes), T6 bead-maze diffusion policy with the Sparsh encoder substituted for the vision encoder (~16% lower trajectory error, mazes only partially completed); +95.1% average over end-to-end; limitations: datasets dominated by "discrete contact interactions", compounding error in real rollouts — [arXiv 2410.24090](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.24090); [project](https://sparsh-ssl.github.io/); [GitHub](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sparsh).
- **Sparsh-X / Tactile Beyond Pixels** (Higuera, Sharma, Fan, Bodduluri, Boots, Kaess, Lambeta, Wu, Liu, Hogan, Mukadam; arXiv June 2025): multisensory SSL on Digit 360 fusing **image, audio, motion (IMU) and pressure** from ~1M contact-rich interactions "at diverse temporal and spatial scales"; +63% policy success over image-only tactile in IL, +90% robustness in object-state recovery, +48% in physical-property inference; per-modality sampling rates not given in abstract — [arXiv 2506.14754](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14754).
- **Digit 360** (Lambeta et al.; Meta; arXiv Nov 2024): 7 µm spatial resolution, ~8.3M taxels, force resolution 1.01 mN normal / 1.27 mN shear, **vibration detection up to 10 kHz**, heat and odor, plus "an on-device AI neural network accelerator that … mimics the reflex arc found in humans" — [arXiv 2411.02479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02479); GelSight/Meta press release states it "captures forces as small as 1 millinewton" and >8M taxels — [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/gelsight-meta-ai-release-digit-360-tactile-sensor-for-robotic-fingers/). No slip-detection results for Digit 360 were found.
- **T3 / Transferable Tactile Transformers** (Zhao, Ma, Wang, Adelson; MIT; CoRL 2024): FoTa dataset of >3M points from 13 sensors and 11 tasks; shared trunk + sensor-specific encoders + task-specific decoders; sub-millimeter multi-pin insertion +25% over scratch encoders and +53% over no tactile; temporal/vibration content not specified — [arXiv 2406.13640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13640); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/zhao25c.html).
- **UniTouch / Binding Touch to Everything** (Yang et al.; CVPR 2024): aligns touch embeddings to pretrained ImageBind image embeddings with learnable sensor-specific tokens; grasp prediction, touch QA, zero-shot tasks — [arXiv 2401.18084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.18084).
- **AnyTouch** (Feng et al.; ICLR 2025): "unified static-dynamic representation" across multiple visuo-tactile sensors via masked modeling + multimodal alignment on Touch and Go, VisGel, Cloth, TVL, SSVTP, YCB-Slide, ObjectFolder Real, Octopi, TacQuad; real-world pouring — [GitHub](https://github.com/GeWu-Lab/AnyTouch). **AnyTouch 2** (Feng et al.; ICLR 2026): ToucHD dataset "spanning tactile atomic actions, real-world manipulations, and touch-force paired data"; "fine-grained, force-aware dynamic perception" — [arXiv 2602.09617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09617).
- **Octopi** (Yu, Lin, Xiao, Duan, Soh; RSS 2024): GelSight tactile videos → property predictions in an LLM (PhysiCLeAR dataset) — [arXiv 2405.02794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02794). **Touch100k** (2024/2025): 101,982 visual–tactile observations with GPT-4V multi-granularity text — [arXiv 2406.03813](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.03813v1).
- **UniForce** (Chen et al. incl. Lepora, Luo; arXiv Feb 2026): shared latent force space across GelSight, TacTip and uSkin by jointly modeling image→force and force→image; zero-shot use in a VTLA wiping task without F/T sensors — [arXiv 2602.01153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01153). **TaF-Adapter** (in TaF-VLA): VQ-VAE force codebooks aligned to a causal-transformer tactile encoder over 5-frame windows at 30 Hz — [arXiv 2601.20321](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20321).
- **ViTaMIn** pretrains with masked autoencoding + tactile–visual contrastive alignment; **FreeTacMan** uses CLIP-style contrastive tactile pretraining (+16 pts success, 55%→71%) — [ViTaMIn](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06156); [FreeTacMan](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01941v1).
- **Simulation**: TacEx embeds the GIPC FEM soft-body simulator and Taxim/FOTS renderers into Isaac Sim for GelSight Mini RL (pushing, lifting, pole balancing) and contrasts itself with concurrent TacSL, which "incorporates visuotactile simulation into Isaac Sim" with "a simplified soft contact model" — [arXiv 2411.04776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04776); [GitHub](https://github.com/DH-Ng/TacEx). TLA's dataset used Isaac Gym FEM — [arXiv 2503.08548](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08548v1).
- **Vibration-band learning outside foundation encoders**: SpectRobot converts single-point signals sampled up to 200 kS/s into 224×224 spectrograms (Tukey window, 50% overlap, FFT 64–4096) consumed by an ACT vision encoder — [arXiv 2609.24621](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24621v1); SlipSense fuses a 32×32 piezoresistive array at 240 Hz with a 3-axis MEMS accelerometer at 8 kHz via cross-modal attention (causal prediction at 240 Hz) — [arXiv 2609.15910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15910); VibeCheck uses active piezo acoustic transmission between fingers for contact-state classification feeding an IL peg-insertion policy — [arXiv 2504.15535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15535).
- Survey: "Vibration- and Acoustic-Based Sensing. Although not yet common among the 53 methods included in the TF-ART corpus, dynamic vibrotactile and acoustic sensing … capture short-lived vibrations and acoustic signals generated by sliding, impact, or slip" — [survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07558).

### Inferences
- A tactile encoder trained on 25–60 FPS gel images cannot represent a hammer strike or drill chatter (hundreds of Hz to kHz); any such content must come from a separate high-bandwidth channel (accelerometer, piezo, contact mic, high-rate F/T) with its own encoder — the ACP FFT-spectrogram branch, ManiWAV's AST, and SpectRobot's spectrogram-as-image are the working precedents.
- Sparsh-X is the closest thing to a "vibration-aware foundation encoder", but its published gains are on state recovery and property inference, not on closed-loop force regulation.

### Gaps
- Sparsh-X per-modality sampling rates and whether audio/IMU streams are used at ≥1 kHz in the encoder are not exposed in the abstract.
- T3, UniTouch, Octopi and Touch100k do not state whether any temporal modeling beyond single frames/short clips is used.
- No published evaluation of Sparsh/T3/AnyTouch encoders on tool-use or impact tasks was found.
- No "Digit 360 slip results" (Meta/NVIDIA) could be located; the Digit 360 paper's on-device "reflex arc" accelerator is described but no slip benchmark was retrieved.

## Key Question 4: Tactile data collection at scale (handheld grippers, gloves, UMI variants) — how do they log force/effort?

### Takeaway
Most handheld visuo-tactile devices (FreeTacMan, ViTaMIn, Touch in the Wild, TactAR) log gel images and pose only — force is implicit in gel deformation; the devices that log explicit force are ForceCapture (6-axis F/T at 1 kHz), TacUMI (Bota SensONE F/T at 1 kHz + GelSight Mini at 16.67 Hz), UMI-FT (per-finger CoinFT at 360 Hz, giving both external contact and internal grasp force), TaF-VLA's automated rig (ATI Axia80 + 12×12 pressure matrix), and Feel-the-Force's magnetometer glove (200 Hz). Joint-effort logging comes from teleoperation rigs (T-Rex 300 Hz fingertip forces; CRAFT's leader–follower with synchronized force; Bi-ACT's observer-based torques on a 1 kHz loop).

### Cited Findings
- **FreeTacMan** (Wu, Yu, Ren, Chen, Huang, Gu, Li; OpenDriveLab/SJTU; ICRA 2026): wearable gripper with dual camera-based visuo-tactile sensors (15–20 µm resolution, 358.47 mm² FOV, 18 mm thick, ~$30) at 30 Hz 640×480; fisheye RGB at 30 Hz; OptiTrack at 300 Hz (0.118 mm mean error) synchronized to 30 Hz; logs 1-DoF gripper width and 6-DoF pose; "No explicit force/effort measurement"; policy = ACT with a CLIP-style pretrained tactile encoder, "latency averaging under 20 ms per cycle"; 8 tasks (fragile cup, USB plugging, texture classification, stamp pressing, calligraphy, toothpaste extrusion, tissue, potato chip); 21% vision-only → 55% visuo-tactile → 71% with pretraining; collection throughput 6.58× ALOHA / 7.68× UMI; dataset >3000k image pairs, >10k trajectories, 50 tasks (not released at time of paper); limitation: external base station — [arXiv 2506.01941](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01941v1); [GitHub](https://github.com/OpenDriveLab/FreeTacMan).
- **ViTaMIn** (Liu, Li, Qin, Xu, Abbeel, Chen; Tsinghua/Berkeley; arXiv Apr 2025): AllTact Fin Ray compliant gripper with omnidirectional vision-based tactile sensing (640×480, 30 FPS), GoPro 10 155° at 60 FPS, SLAM tracking (~80% success), ArUco for width, ~1960 g; force logged only via a force gauge in one articulated-object task, otherwise "implicit force feedback through gripper deformation"; diffusion policy at 10 Hz (16 predicted, 10 executed); tasks incl. **knife pulling (dual-arm)** 0.90 and dynamic peg insertion 0.90; 25% of demos suffice with pretraining — [arXiv 2504.06156](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06156). **ViTaMIn-B** adds VR-controller tracking for bimanual collection — [arXiv 2511.05858](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.05858v2).
- **TacUMI** (Cheng et al.; TUM / Agile Robots; arXiv Jan 2026): GelSight Mini at 16.67 Hz, **Bota SensONE 6-D F/T at 1000 Hz**, HTC Vive tracker at 60 Hz, RealSense D435i at 60 Hz, all downsampled to 16.67 Hz; lockable jaw to isolate contact measurement; used for BiLSTM task segmentation (>90% accuracy) on cable mounting; policy learning is future work; handheld ~70 s vs ~4 min teleop — [arXiv 2601.14550](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14550v1).
- **UMI-FT** (Song lab): per-finger CoinFT 6-axis F/T at 360 Hz "capturing both external contact forces and internal grasp force that human demonstrators naturally experience"; ablations show "explicit finger-level force data" helps forceful tasks such as whiteboard wiping and zucchini skewering — [arXiv 2601.09988](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09988). Yunzhu Li publicly noted the original UMI's "key limitation is that force-related data wasn't captured" — [X post](https://x.com/YunzhuLiYZ/status/1945901122738426078).
- **ForceCapture** (ForceMimic): 6-axis F/T at 1000 Hz with gravity compensation, T265 SLAM at 200 Hz; operator peeled a zucchini in ~5 min vs >13 min with force-feedback teleop — [arXiv 2410.07554](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07554v1).
- **TactAR** (RDP): Quest 3 AR rendering of 3-D tactile deformation fields attached to the virtual end-effector, streaming RGB and tactile video; ~$500 headset — [arXiv 2503.02881](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02881).
- **Touch in the Wild** (Zhu, Huang, Li): "portable, lightweight gripper with integrated tactile sensors" for in-the-wild synchronized visuo-tactile collection; sensor specs and force logging not in abstract — [arXiv 2507.15062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15062).
- **Feel the Force glove** (Pinto lab): custom AnySkin-inspired magnetometer glove on the thumb underside at 200 Hz (downsampled to 30 Hz); hand pose from stereo triangulated keypoints; robot gripper carries a matching tactile sensor for force replication — [project](https://feel-the-force-ftf.github.io).
- **TaF-VLA acquisition rig**: 5 Dynamixel XM540 servos, cam-follower roll/pitch ±15°, compliant Z stage, 60+ indenters; 100,000 synchronized frames/hour; >10M frames; sensors include ATI Axia80 and a 12×12 piezoresistive matrix — [arXiv 2601.20321](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20321).
- **T-Rex dataset**: 100 h of tactile-synchronized bimanual teleoperation with Sharpa Wave hands, 300 Hz 6-D fingertip forces, 200+ objects, 22 motor primitives — [arXiv 2606.17055](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17055).
- **CRAFT**: "homologous leader-follower teleoperation system that collects synchronized vision, language, and force data" — [arXiv 2602.12532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12532). **ForceVLA-Data**: VR teleop on Flexiv Rizon with wrist F/T, 244 trajectories — [arXiv 2505.22159](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22159v1).
- **DexUMI** (Xu, Zhang, Hou, Xu, Fan, Veloso, Song; arXiv May 2025): wearable hand exoskeleton + robot-hand video inpainting; 86% average success on two hands; the abstract mentions "direct haptic feedback" but does not describe tactile/force logging — [arXiv 2505.21864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21864).
- **Gloves / hands**: MANUS reports HRDexDB with "1,400 grasping episodes across 100 objects and four embodiments, with synchronized 3D hand trajectories, object 6D poses, tactile sensing, egocentric RGBD streams" — [MANUS](https://www.manus-meta.com/use-cases/how-manus-gloves-enable-paired-human-robot-data-collection-in-hrdexdb); the T-800 800 Hz data glove targets pose, not force — [arXiv 2603.26403](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.26403); DEX-Mouse is a low-cost dexterous-hand interface "with force feedback" — [arXiv 2604.15013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.15013).
- **Whole-body**: TWINS pairs a wearable dual-arm device with distributed tactile sensors on chest/arms and an isomorphic robot for body-contact IL — [arXiv 2608.01733](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01733); TACT equips the RHP7 Kaleido humanoid upper body with tactile sensors for teleop-IL — [arXiv 2506.15146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15146).
- **FACTR 2 teleop**: learned external-torque estimate from motor current gives force feedback "rated comparable to dedicated sensors" in a user study — [arXiv 2606.12406](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12406v1).

### Inferences
- For a hammer/drill dataset the relevant precedents are the F/T-instrumented handhelds (ForceCapture 1 kHz, TacUMI 1 kHz, UMI-FT 360 Hz), because gel-only devices lose the high-bandwidth force transient; note every one of them downsamples to ≤30 Hz before training.
- Per-finger F/T (UMI-FT) is the only handheld that separates internal grasp force from external tool reaction — directly relevant to regulating grip force during impacts.

### Gaps
- No handheld tactile device logs accelerometer/vibration channels; none reports collecting hammering or drilling demonstrations.
- DexUMI's tactile/force logging could not be confirmed from the abstract.
- "Meta digit gloves", "ManiVID" and Tesla/Sharpa-style glove collection did not resolve to primary sources within the available search budget (only the Sharpa Wave hand in T-Rex was verified).

## Key Question 5: Grip-force regulation in learned policies — slip-driven regrasp, tactile force regulation, VLAs with gripper-force heads

### Takeaway
Learned grip-force regulation exists but is mostly outside VLAs: FARM (target grip force + 25 Hz PID), UMI-FT (grasp force + stiffness → 30 Hz gripper admittance), Feel-the-Force (desired force → PD closure), the RA-L gentle-grasping predictor, TAC-LOCO (RL, −47% grasp force at <1% drops), plus model-based/learned slip detectors (Sparsh T2, SlipSense 23 ms latency, "Tracing Energy Flow"). Among VLAs, Tactile-VLA regulates internal grasp force through its gripper-width channel, Tabero emits decoupled force commands, HapticVLA penalizes excessive grasp force via rewards, and AT-VLA reports slip failures because grip firmness is not regulated.

### Cited Findings
- **Tactile-VLA**: "the gripper width is used in parallel to control the internal grasping force" under a hybrid controller; object-adaptive force across 12 objects; fragile OOD grasp 90% vs 0–10% — [arXiv 2507.09160](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09160).
- **FARM**: predicts "target grip force" executed by a PID with anti-windup; force loop 25 Hz; grape picking (delicate) and screw tightening (dynamic force adaptation) at 95–100% — [arXiv 2510.13324](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13324v1).
- **UMI-FT**: outputs grasp force and stiffness; "policies reliably regulate external contact forces and internal grasp forces"; gripper force controller at 30 Hz — [arXiv 2601.09988](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09988).
- **Feel the Force**: PD controller "adjusts the gripper closure iteratively until the measured force matches the policy's predicted value"; failures in baselines were "overforce-induced object damage" — [project](https://feel-the-force-ftf.github.io).
- **Tabero**: >70% average grip-force reduction under "gentle" language via decoupled force–position commands — [arXiv 2605.27886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27886).
- **HapticVLA**: training-time tactile rewards "penalizing excessive grasping force"; no tactile at inference — [arXiv 2603.15257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15257).
- **Learning Gentle Grasping from Human-Free Force Control Demonstration** (Li, Zhang, Li, Jiang; RA-L Mar 2025): reference force curves auto-generated from objects with known contact characteristics; Dual-CNN with a physics-based mechanics module predicts target grasp forces from vision-based tactile — [arXiv 2409.10371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10371).
- **TAC-LOCO** (Hu et al.; arXiv Jul 2026): RL whole-body policy with tactile-array input learns to moderate grasp force, "47% reduction in grasping force" with <1% drop rate under load changes — [arXiv 2607.10132](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=%28all:%22grip%20force%22%20OR%20all:%22grasp%20force%22%20OR%20all:%22gripping%20force%22%29%20AND%20all:slip%20AND%20%28all:%22diffusion%20policy%22%20OR%20all:%22imitation%20learning%22%20OR%20all:vision-language-action%20OR%20all:%22reinforcement%20learning%22%29&max_results=20&sortBy=submittedDate&sortOrder=descending).
- **Tracing Energy Flow** (Kuo, Shin, Matsubara; arXiv Dec 2025/Sep 2026): treats the object as a "virtual energy container"; the discrepancy between applied power and retained energy is the slip signal; model-based learning "within minutes" reduces slippage under motion with rolling contacts — [arXiv 2512.21043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21043).
- **SlipSense** (Jian et al.; arXiv Sep 2026): 32×32 piezoresistive array at 240 Hz + MEMS accelerometer at 8 kHz; 76% of slips detected within 23.1 ms; 96.7% macro-F1, <1.6% false positives; zero-shot UMI→Tesollo hand; no grip-force loop reported — [arXiv 2609.15910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15910).
- **TactileReflex**: ~12 Hz slip-suppression / force-protection reflexes on visuo-tactile proxies; dynamic pouring 9/10 vs 0/10 — [arXiv 2605.23568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23568).
- **AnySkin / ViSk** (Pinto lab): magnetic skin with cross-instance generalization; slip detection and policy learning; ViSk treats skin readings as extra transformer tokens, +27.5% over vision-only and optical tactile on card swiping, plug/USB insertion, bookshelf retrieval — [AnySkin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08276); [ViSk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17246). No explicit force regulation loop is described.
- **Origin work**: Calandra et al. 2018 (RA-L) learned action-conditional regrasp outcomes from GelSight, "reducing the amount of force applied at the fingers, while maintaining competitive performance" (6,450 trials) — [arXiv 1805.11085](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.11085).
- **Non-learned but fast**: Waltersson & Karayiannidis use per-finger force and sliding-velocity sensing with "rapid closed-loop, low-level force control" and four slip controllers — [arXiv 2410.19660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19660); a Nature Machine Intelligence 2025 study shows trajectory modulation can outperform grip-force modulation for slip control — [Nature MI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-025-01062-2).
- **VLA failure evidence**: AT-VLA underperformed on Unscrew Lid "due to occasional gripper slippage when grip firmness isn't optimal" — [arXiv 2605.07308](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07308).
- Sparsh's 80 ms window was selected to match "the reaction time that humans need to adjust grip force" — [arXiv 2410.24090](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.24090).

### Inferences
- Every learned grip-force regulator found runs its force loop at 12–30 Hz (FARM 25 Hz, UMI-FT 30 Hz, TactileReflex 12 Hz); the fastest *detector* (SlipSense, 23 ms) is not yet closed into a controller. For hammer recoil or drill bit bind-up, a sub-10 ms regulation loop would have to be hand-designed below the learned policy.
- The design most compatible with the user's architecture is UMI-FT's: the slow policy emits (pose, virtual target, stiffness, grasp force) and dedicated 500 Hz / 30 Hz controllers enforce them.

### Gaps
- No learned policy was found that regulates grip force *during* an impact or vibration event; slip work is dominated by lifting/pouring/tilting.
- Digit 360 "slip results" and ReSkin/AnySkin-based *force regulation* (as opposed to detection) were not found.
- Rates for Tabero, HapticVLA, gentle-grasping RA-L and Tracing-Energy-Flow controllers are not stated in the fetched abstracts.

## Key Question 6: Joint effort / proprioceptive force as a policy input — who uses joint torque or motor current, and does it help?

### Takeaway
Joint-effort inputs are increasingly common and there is now direct evidence that they help contact-rich tasks: TA-VLA (motor-current torque → single decoder token, 15–18/20 vs 0–5/20), FACTR 2 (learned external torque from current beats raw current: 0.567 vs 0.431 on NIST insertion, 0.525 vs 0.440 on cap screwing, at 100 Hz), Bi-ACT (observer torques in and out at ~100 Hz), RDP (120 Hz Flexiv joint torque), FILIC and FWBC-VLA (sensorless estimates), and "Compliance for Free" (joint-torque sensing for stiffness labels). The survey counts 9 of 53 methods using joint-torque sensing and warns that raw torque mixes motion and contact loads and is embodiment-dependent.

### Cited Findings
- **TA-VLA**: torque from motor currents on ALOHA arms; 2 s history token in the decoder; auxiliary torque prediction; contact-rich 15–18/20 vs 0–5/20; noted drift/calibration risk — [arXiv 2509.07962](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07962).
- **FACTR 2** (Oh, Liu, Tao, Han, Shaw, Funabashi, Salakhutdinov, Pathak; CMU/Waseda; arXiv June 2026): NEXT = LSTM trained on 10 min of free motion predicts free-space torque; external torque = measured motor torque − prediction; L1 error 0.547 Nm (contact) / 0.414 Nm (free); runs at **100 Hz** (568 Hz possible on CPU); τ̂ext embedded by MLP as observation tokens; "raw joint current … the policy must implicitly disentangle contact-induced torque from free-space dynamics"; learned torque > raw current (NIST insertion 0.567 vs 0.431; cap screwing 0.525 vs 0.440); phase-based upsampling of pre-contact segments 5× gives 0.818 vs 0.670; tasks LEGO, NIST belt, NIST insertion, **tool cleanup (screwdriver + case)**, **cap screwing**; >17% over prior force-aware methods; "No hammering or impact tasks"; limitation: robot-specific, depends on torque constant K — [arXiv 2606.12406](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12406v1). The same paper summarizes BiACT as using "joint torque as policy input within bilateral control" and CompACT as conditioning "the policy on end-effector force".
- **Bi-ACT**: DOB/RFOB torque estimates from encoders; 15-D follower state incl. force; predicts leader torques; 1 kHz loop, ~100 Hz policy — [arXiv 2401.17698](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.17698).
- **RDP**: Flexiv Rizon 4 joint torque at 120 Hz as part of the observation (6-D wrench concatenated) — [arXiv 2503.02881](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02881).
- **FILIC**: EE force from joint torques via Jacobian + digital-twin compensation; "replacing raw joint torque with estimated end-effector force consistently improves policy performance" — [arXiv 2509.17053](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.17053).
- **FWBC-VLA**: sensorless residual-torque estimator tokens into the VLA — [arXiv 2609.03889](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03889). **Compliance for Free**: joint-torque sensing + bilateral leader as equilibrium measurement → identifiable per-axis stiffness — [arXiv 2609.19976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19976). **Energy-regularized IL** builds a mechanical-work proxy from joint torque × displacement (RLBench) — [arXiv 2609.18164](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=%28all:%22joint%20effort%22%20OR%20all:%22joint%20torque%22%20OR%20all:%22motor%20current%22%29%20AND%20%28all:%22imitation%20learning%22%20OR%20all:%22diffusion%20policy%22%20OR%20all:vision-language-action%29%20AND%20all:manipulation&max_results=20&sortBy=submittedDate&sortOrder=descending).
- **Humanoids**: Touch Dreaming (HTD) predicts "future hand-joint forces and future tactile latents" as auxiliary targets; 90.9% relative success improvement over five real tasks; latent tactile prediction +30% relative over raw — [arXiv 2604.13015](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13015). TACT feeds upper-body tactile arrays + joint positions + vision into a humanoid IL policy, improving robustness of broad-contact manipulation — [arXiv 2506.15146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15146).
- Survey: "Raw joint torque includes loads caused by both robot motion and external contact"; "Wrist wrench and joint torque measurements are embodiment-dependent"; joint-torque sensors "must be integrated into the actuators rather than added afterward" — [survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07558).

### Inferences
- The evidence favors feeding a *contact-isolated* effort signal (learned external torque or estimated EE wrench) rather than raw current; for a drill whose motor may or may not be engaged, this argues for an explicit free-motion/tool-load model so that the policy sees load changes, not actuator dynamics.
- Joint effort has only been used at ≤120 Hz as an observation and ~100 Hz as an output (Bi-ACT); no work uses joint effort to detect or respond to impacts.

### Gaps
- CompACT itself was not retrieved (only FACTR 2's characterization); its rates and outputs are unverified.
- No humanoid policy conditioned on *joint effort/torque* (rather than tactile arrays) was found for contact-rich manipulation.

## Key Question 7: Frequency — sensor sampling and policy inference rates; anyone feeding tactile at >200 Hz into a learned policy; spectrogram/FFT tactile features

### Takeaway
Stated policy rates cluster at 7–30 Hz (FARM 7, VLA-Touch 8, FoAR/ImplicitRDP/ViTaMIn 10, TA-VLA ~11, RDP-fast 24, FA-RDP-fast/T-Rex 30, SpectRobot ~30, Bi-ACT ~100, TacDiffusion 50–500); controllers below them run at 100 Hz–1 kHz. Only T-Rex (300 Hz tactile → VQ tokens), SlipSense (8 kHz accelerometer, perception only), ACP (7 kHz F/T with an FFT-spectrogram encoder), SpectRobot (200 kS/s → spectrogram images) and ManiWAV (48 kHz audio → log-mel/AST) put >200 Hz contact signals into learned models, and all of them compress the high-rate stream into a slow token/image rather than raising control bandwidth.

- Survey on architecture vs latency: "bridging high-level semantic reasoning with the high-frequency, low-latency continuous control required for precision contact tasks remains a central architectural challenge"; multi-phase systems let "a lower-level refinement policy or controller … make rapid adjustments" and "Future systems should further formalize this division of labor, with slow reasoning modules handling task planning and fast reactive modules handling contact adaptation" — [survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07558).

### Inferences
- The fastest *learned* loops on real robots are Bi-ACT (~100 Hz, tiny 4-DoF arm) and TacDiffusion (50–500 Hz on a wrench-domain MLP diffusion); every vision-conditioned policy sits at ≤30 Hz. A hammer strike (ms-scale) or drill chatter (100s of Hz) therefore cannot be *reacted to* by any published learned policy; only the hand-designed controller beneath it can.
- The practical precedent for a high-frequency tactile channel is "spectrogram-as-image into the existing vision encoder" (SpectRobot, ACP-FFT, ManiWAV): it needs no new architecture and preserves 2–3 s context, which SpectRobot shows matters more than bandwidth above 10 kHz.

### Gaps
- No policy learning work feeds raw tactile at >200 Hz into the *action* loop; T-Rex is the sole VLA with 300 Hz tactile, but its policy is 30 Hz.
- Rates are "not stated" for many VLAs (see KQ1 gaps) and for ACP/UMI-FT policy inference.
- MiTaS's Evetac rate was not exposed in the abstract; PolyTouch's acoustic sampling rate is not in the abstract — [PolyTouch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19341).

## Key Question 9: Honest assessment — how each family handles (or ignores) high-frequency events, control rate and grip-force regulation

### Takeaway
By the (i)/(ii)/(iii) classification: most tactile VLAs are (i) with ~10 Hz position chunks and no grip-force channel; the reactive IL family is (ii) with 10–30 Hz fast loops that refine *positions*; the force-output family (TacDiffusion, ACP/UMI-FT, ForceMimic, FARM, Feel-the-Force, Bi-ACT, Tactile-VLA, Tabero) is (iii) and pushes bandwidth into 25 Hz–1 kHz controllers. High-frequency events are handled by no one at the policy level; the only mechanisms that touch them are (a) high-rate controllers executing policy-specified force/stiffness and (b) spectrogram encoders that summarize vibration for the slow policy.

- Explicit statements of the limitation: TaF-VLA's receding horizon "limit[s] high-frequency dynamic events" — [source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20321); VLA-Touch "does not yet fully leverage high-frequency tactile signals (≥25 Hz)" — [source](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.17294v1); RDP's "fast policy cannot process high-frequency visual inputs" and "system latency remains relevant" — [source](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02881); FoAR deploys "simple position control" and defers "compliance control and hybrid force/position control" — [source](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15753v1); ForceMimic: force-as-input models "struggled when interactive forces during execution deviated significantly from training data" — [source](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07554v1); SpectRobot: piezo sensing gives no static normal force, and "temporal context and bandwidth not independently controlled" — [source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24621v1); survey: diffusion latency "makes direct deployment in high-frequency closed-loop control challenging" — [survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07558).

### Inferences
- For the user's architecture (slow VLA/world model + high-frequency controller on vision + tactile + joint state + effort), the literature supports: (1) UMI-FT/ACP-style action parameterization (pose, virtual target, stiffness, grasp force) so the fast controller has force-domain setpoints; (2) TacDiffusion's evidence that a wrench-domain policy can run at 50–500 Hz when it is a small MLP with no vision; (3) T-Rex's asynchronous 300 Hz tactile VQ tokens and AT-VLA's 3:1 slow/fast streams as templates for decoupling rates inside one model; (4) FACTR 2's learned external-torque estimate as the effort input; (5) SpectRobot/ACP-FFT spectrogram channels for engagement/impact detection.
- What the literature does not provide: any learned component that reacts within the ~1–10 ms of an impact, any grip-force regulation validated under impact or vibration, or any tactile/force dataset for hammering or drilling. Those must be engineered below the learned stack and evaluated from scratch.
