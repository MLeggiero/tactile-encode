Control architecture · rev 0

Kilohertz Tool Loop

Three control tiers plus the plant, drawn with their real rates and the signals that cross each boundary. The slow planner never sits on the reactive path. Everything that must respond to a 3 ms hammer pulse or a cam-out lives in the two lower tiers, and the learned reactive layer only ever hands the 1 kHz controller a pose, a stiffness, a feedforward wrench, and a grasp force.

1. Multi-rate dataflow

Read top to bottom for commands and bottom to top for measurements. The teal bus on the left is the kilohertz sensor path; it feeds the reactive layer and the controller directly and reaches the planner only as compressed tokens.

2. Reactive layer, one tick during a hammer strike

This is what runs every 5–10 ms. The branch is chosen by the contact-phase estimate, not by the clock, because the strike arrives when the swing says it does, not when the plan said it would. Grip is set before the impact from the predicted load; feedback after the impact only fixes the next strike.

3. Drill regime state machine

For the driver the fast loop is a supervisor over regimes rather than a strike schedule. Every transition is triggered by a signal signature the reactive layer can see within one revolution, and every state carries the action the controller applies while in it.

4. Timing budget for one strike

Times are relative to the actual contact instant. The budget shows why each responsibility sits where it does: nothing above the controller can act inside the pulse, and nothing below the planner can decide where the next strike goes.

6. Watchdogs and fallbacks

  • L3 stale > 500 ms: hold the last schedule, finish the current phase, start no new swing.
  • L2 stale > 20 ms: controller drops to conservative mode, holds pose, sets grip to the safe hold value, zeroes feedforward.
  • F/T or accelerometer dropout: impact logic falls back to the momentum observer alone; slip detector disabled; planner told to stop swinging.
  • drop detected (grip force collapses while the pad accelerometer spikes): freeze the arm, open nothing, report.
  • kickback (reaction torque above limit): cut the tool trigger first, then back off along the bit axis.
  • tool drift beyond bound between strikes: no further strikes until the planner issues a regrasp phase.
  • Joint torque and velocity saturation and the hardware E-stop sit below all of this and are never overridden by a learned layer.
Rates and latencies follow the research report in this repository: planner latency 100–250 ms, reactive inference under 5 ms, controller at 1 kHz, impact pulse about 3 ms, human anticipatory grip lead about 150 ms and grip peak 50–70 ms after peak load. Simulation targets are a 0.1–0.25 ms physics step and a phenomenological drill plant calibrated from the screwdriving and machining datasets in the catalogs.
